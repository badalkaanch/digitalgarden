### 📜 🕒 [01:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=104s) **2.0 Overview of the Indian Constitution**
*   Considered one of the **lengthiest Constitutions** in the world.
*   **Articles:**
    *   Spread across **395 articles** (numerically).
    *   Actual number of articles is over 450 (e.g., Article 21 and 21 A are distinct but numbered under 21; similarly 51 and 51 A).
*   **Schedules:**
    *   Original Constitution (1950): **8 schedules**.
    *   Currently: **12 schedules** (4 added over time).
*   **Parts:**
    *   Spread across **22 parts** (numerically).
    *   Actual number of parts is 25 (e.g., Part IX - Panchayats, Part IXA - Municipalities, Part IXB - Cooperative Societies).
*   **Amendments:**
    *   Amended **106 times** as of the recording.
    *   **Last amendment (106 th):** Women's Reservation Act (2024 mentioned, likely meant 2023).
        *   Provides reservation for women in Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies.
        *   **Not yet in force** because it requires:
            1.  Census to be carried out.
            2.  Delimitation exercise based on the census.
        *   May come into force beyond 2029.

### 🇮🇳 🕒 [04:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=250s) **3.0 The Preamble**
*   Our constitution begins with a Preamble, similar to the American Constitution.
*   **"We the people of India..."**:
    *   The Supreme Court in **Kesavananda Bharati vs. State of Kerala (1973)** held that the Preamble is part of the Constitution.
    *   Creates a **legal fiction**: though drafted and adopted by the Constituent Assembly, it's framed as if adopted by the people.
*   **Source of Authority**: The Preamble indicates the Constitution derives its authority from **"We the people of India"**.
*   **Nature of Indian State**:
    *   **Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic**.
    *   These terms are written in capital letters in the Preamble without commas, implying they form a **composite whole** or an integrated phrase.
    *   India cannot be Sovereign without being Socialist, Secular, Democratic, and Republic.
*   **Objectives**:
    *   Liberty, Equality, Fraternity, Dignity of an individual.
*   **Significance of the Preamble**:
    *   Though **not enforceable** in a court of law.
    *   Can be used to **interpret ambiguous provisions** of the Constitution.
    *   Example: In 2017, a nine-judge bench of the Supreme Court, in the **Right to Privacy case**, referred to the Preamble's mention of "Dignity of an individual" to conclude that Right to Privacy is a fundamental right under Article 21. SC said dignity is not possible without privacy.
*   **Date of Adoption**:
    *   Adopted on **26 th November 1949**.
    *   Came into force on **26 th January 1950**.
    *   The choice of 26 th January is linked to the **Purna Swaraj resolution** passed by the Congress in 1929 at the Lahore session.
*   **Amendment to the Preamble**:
    *   Amended once by the **42 nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976**.
    *   Terms added:
        1.  **Socialist**
        2.  **Secular**
        3.  **Integrity** (Original: "Unity of the nation", changed to "Unity and Integrity of the nation").

### 🗺️ 🕒 [08:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=512s) **4.0 Part I: The Union and its Territory (Articles 1-4)**

#### 🇮🇳 🕒 [08:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=525s) **4.1 Article 1: Name and territory of the Union**
*   `**Article 1 (1)**`: **"India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States."**
    *   Dual name for the country: **India** and **Bharat**.
    *   Dr. Ambedkar used the term **"Union of States"** instead of "Federation of States" because:
        1.  The Indian federation is not the result of an agreement among states.
        2.  No state has the right to **secede** from the Union.
*   Names of states and union territories are specified in the **First Schedule** of the Constitution.
    *   Currently: 28 States and 8 Union Territories.
*   `**Article 1 (3)**`: The territory of India shall comprise:
    *   The territories of the States.
    *   The Union territories specified in the First Schedule.
    *   Such other territories as may be **acquired**.
*   The term "Union of States" in Article 1 (1) refers **only to the 28 States**, not the Union Territories, as UTs are directly controlled by the Center and don't share a federal relationship.

#### ➕ 🕒 [11:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=665s) **4.2 Article 2: Admission or establishment of new States**
*   Parliament may by law **admit into the Union, or establish, new States** on such terms and conditions as it thinks fit.
*   Pertains to states not already in existence or part of India.
*   Historically, for acquisitions like Sikkim, Daman & Diu, Nagar Haveli, Puducherry, Goa, **Article 368** was used, not Article 2.
    *   Sikkim was first made an associate State, then a full-fledged State via Article 368.
    *   **Chandranagore** was perhaps the only entity added using Article 2.
*   The phrase **"as it thinks fit"** means a new state admitted under Article 2 cannot claim parity with existing states regarding legislative powers (e.g., items under Schedule 7).

#### 🔄 🕒 [11:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=701s) **4.3 Article 3: Formation of new States and alteration of areas, boundaries or names of existing States**
*   Deals with **existing states**: bifurcation, trifurcation, merging, increasing/decreasing area, altering boundaries, changing names.
*   Considered a **centralizing feature** of Indian federalism as this power rests with the Parliament.
*   **Procedure for Reorganization under Article 3**:
    1.  A bill for reorganization requires **prior approval of the President**.
    2.  Before giving approval, the President **must refer the bill to the legislature of the affected state (s)** for expressing their views within a specified period.
    3.  The **views of the state legislature are not binding** on the President or Parliament.
        *   Example: Andhra Pradesh reorganization in 2014.
*   **Ambiguity and Interpretation (re: J&K Reorganization)**:
    *   Former CJI Chandrachud highlighted ambiguity in Article 3.
    *   `**Explanation I to Article 3**`: "State" in clauses (a) to (e) includes a Union Territory.
    *   `**Proviso to Article 3**`: "State" does *not* include a Union Territory for the purpose of mandatory referral to the state legislature.
        *   Thus, for reorganizing a UT (e.g., Delhi, Puducherry, J&K), President need not send the bill to its legislature.
    *   **J&K Reorganization Case**:
        *   Supreme Court upheld carving out Ladakh as a UT from J&K state under Article 3.
        *   The question of whether Parliament can convert an *entire* state into a UT (or multiple UTs) was left undecided.
        *   SC relied on the Solicitor General's assurance that UT status for J&K is temporary and statehood will be restored.
        *   If an entire state can be converted to UTs, Article 1 (1) might effectively become "Union of Union Territories," violating federalism (a basic structure).

#### 📜 🕒 [19:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=1170s) **4.4 Article 4: Laws made under Articles 2 and 3 to provide for the amendment of the First and the Fourth Schedules and supplemental, incidental and consequential matters**
*   Laws made under Article 2 or 3 will necessitate changes to:
    *   **Schedule 1** (names and territories of States and UTs).
    *   **Schedule 4** (allocation of seats in Rajya Sabha to States).
*   Article 4 states that such changes (amendments to Schedule 1 and 4) are considered **incidental and consequential matters**.
*   They **shall not be deemed as an amendment to the Constitution** for the purposes of Article 368.
*   This means such laws can be passed by a **simple majority** (ordinary law), not requiring a special majority.

### 👤 🕒 [21:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=1317s) **5.0 Part II: Citizenship (Articles 5-11)**
*   Deals with citizenship from **Articles 5 to 11**.
*   **Article 11**: Gives Parliament the **real power to enact laws** to regulate all matters related to citizenship.
    *   Parliament passed the **Citizenship Act, 1955**, which has been amended multiple times.
    *   Latest amendment: **Citizenship Amendment Act (CAA), 2019**.
        *   Applies to undocumented migrants from Pakistan, Afghanistan, Bangladesh.
        *   Who entered India on or before December 31, 2014.
        *   Belonging to six religious minorities (Hindus, Christians, Buddhists, Jains, Sikhs, Parsis).
        *   They shall not be deemed illegal migrants and are eligible for citizenship via **naturalization**.
*   Part II primarily deals with **citizenship at the time of the commencement of the Constitution** (26 th January 1950).
    *   However, citizenship provisions came into force on **26 th November 1949** itself.
*   **Key Articles**:
    *   🌍 🕒 [24:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=1487s) **Article 5: Citizenship at the commencement of the Constitution**
        *   Persons born in India, or whose parents were born in India, and residing in India for 5 years. (Based on *jus soli* - law of the soil).
    *   🇵🇰➡️🇮🇳 🕒 [25:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=1526s) **Article 6: Rights of citizenship of certain persons who have migrated to India from Pakistan**
        *   Cut-off date: Migration before **19 th July 1948**.
    *   🇮🇳➡️🇵🇰 🕒 [26:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=1595s) **Article 7: Rights of citizenship of certain migrants to Pakistan**
        *   Those who migrated from India to Pakistan after **1 st March 1947** are not citizens of India.
        *   If they wished to return, they had to do so before **19 th July 1948** under a permit for permanent return.
    *   🌏 🕒 [28:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=1700s) **Article 8: Rights of citizenship of certain persons of Indian origin residing outside India**
        *   Can register as citizens at an Indian Embassy/Consulate.
    *   🚫🤝 🕒 [28:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=1738s) **Article 9: Persons voluntarily acquiring citizenship of a foreign State not to be citizens**
        *   Establishes **single citizenship**. India does not recognize dual citizenship. This is a constitutional provision, not just from the Citizenship Act.
    *   ✅ 🕒 [29:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=1786s) **Article 10: Continuance of the rights of citizenship**
        *   Persons granted citizenship under Part II continue to be citizens, subject to laws made by Parliament under Article 11.
    *   ⚖️ 🕒 [22:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=1331s) **Article 11: Parliament to regulate the right of citizenship by law**
        *   Parliament has the power to make any provision regarding acquisition and termination of citizenship.

### 🛡️ 🕒 [30:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=1832s) **6.0 Part III: Fundamental Rights (Articles 12-35)**

#### 🏢 🕒 [30:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=1846s) **6.1 Article 12: Definition of "State"**
*   Fundamental Rights (FRs) are limitations on the powers of the "State".
*   Article 12 defines "State" for the purpose of Part III. It includes:
    1.  The Government and Parliament of India.
    2.  The Government and the Legislature of each of the States.
    3.  All **local authorities** within the territory of India or under the control of the Government of India.
    4.  All **other authorities** within the territory of India or under the control of the Government of India.
*   The term **"other authority"** is not defined in the Constitution and has been interpreted by the Supreme Court.

#### ⚖️ 🕒 [32:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=1943s) **6.2 Article 13: Laws inconsistent with or in derogation of the fundamental rights**
*   `**Article 13 (1)**`: Pre-constitutional laws inconsistent with FRs shall be void to the extent of inconsistency after the commencement of the Constitution.
*   `**Article 13 (2)**`: The State **shall not make any law** which takes away or abridges FRs. Any law made in contravention shall be void.
    *   "Law" includes ordinance, order, by-law, rule, regulation, notification, custom, or usage having the force of law.
*   FRs can be modified/abridged by Parliament only through a **Constitutional Amendment Act under Article 368**, not an ordinary law.
*   **Grounds for challenging laws**:
    *   **Ordinary Law**: Can be challenged on two grounds:
        1.  Violation of **Fundamental Rights** (explicit in Article 13).
        2.  **Legislative Competence** (e.g., state legislature making law on a Union List subject).
        *   *Cannot* be challenged for violating Basic Structure (Basic Structure doctrine applies to Constitutional Amendments).
    *   **Constitutional Amendment Act**: Can be challenged if it violates the **Basic Structure** of the Constitution (as per Kesavananda Bharati case, 1973).

#### ⚖️ 🕒 [38:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=2287s) **6.3 Right to Equality (Articles 14-18)**

##### ✨ 🕒 [38:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=2300s) **6.3.1 Article 14: Equality before law**
*   "The State shall not deny to **any person** (citizens and aliens) equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws within the territory of India."
*   Includes **Right against Arbitrariness**: Legislature cannot pass an arbitrary law.

##### 🚫 🕒 [39:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=2358s) **6.3.2 Article 15: Prohibition of discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth**
*   Available **only to citizens**.
*   Prohibits State discrimination *only* on grounds of **religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth (RRCSP)**.
    *   Discrimination on other grounds (e.g., age, educational qualification, medical grounds) may be permissible if reasonable.
*   `**Article 15 (2)**`: Prohibits discrimination by State and private individuals regarding access to shops, public restaurants, hotels, places of public entertainment, or use of wells, tanks, bathing ghats, roads, and places of public resort maintained wholly or partly out of State funds or dedicated to the use of the general public. (Helps fight untouchability).
*   **Exceptions (State can make special provisions for)**:
    *   Women and children (`Clause 3`).
    *   Socially and Educationally Backward Classes (SEBCs), Scheduled Castes (SCs), Scheduled Tribes (STs) (`Clause 4`, added by 1 st Amendment, 1951). This allows for reservation in educational institutions.
    *   Economically Weaker Sections (EWS) (`Clause 6`, added by 103 rd Amendment, 2019).

##### 💼 🕒 [43:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=2591s) **6.3.3 Article 16: Equality of opportunity in matters of public employment**
*   Available **only to citizens**.
*   Prohibits State discrimination *only* on grounds of **religion, race, caste, sex, descent, place of birth, residence (RRCDSPR)** or any of them. (7 grounds).
    *   Discrimination on other grounds (e.g., age, educational qualification, medical grounds) may be permissible.
*   **Domicile**:
    *   Constitution provides for **single citizenship** and (as per SC interpretation) **single domicile** (domicile within the territory of India).
    *   Domicile can be a basis for reservation in educational institutions (not public employment generally).
*   **Exceptions**:
    *   `**Article 16 (3)**`: Parliament (not State Legislature) can prescribe **residence as a qualification** for certain state-level jobs (not Union-level).
        *   Example: Article 371 J for Hyderabad-Karnataka (Kalyana Karnataka) region.
    *   Reservation in public employment for SCs, STs, OBCs (SEBCs).
    *   Reservation for EWS (103 rd Amendment).
    *   **Article 16 (5)**: Laws can require an incumbent of an office connected with a religious or denominational institution to be a person professing that particular religion or belonging to that denomination (e.g., appointments to Wakf Board, Gurdwara Prabandhak Committee, TTD).

##### ❌ 🕒 [49:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=2963s) **6.3.4 Article 17: Abolition of Untouchability**
*   "Untouchability" is abolished and its practice in any form is forbidden.
*   The term **"untouchability" (in quotes in the Constitution) is not defined**. It refers to the historical practice based on caste, not literal untouchability (e.g., due to contagious disease).
*   Enforcement of any disability arising out of untouchability shall be an **offence punishable in accordance with law**.
    *   Parliament has enacted laws like the Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955, and the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989.
    *   Atrocities Act includes **presumption of guilt** (burden of proof on accused).

##### 🏅 🕒 [52:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=3121s) **6.3.5 Article 18: Abolition of titles**
*   Prohibits the State from conferring titles, except **military or academic distinctions**.
    *   Awards like Bharat Ratna, Padma Vibhushan, Padma Bhushan, Padma Shri are **not titles** within the meaning of Article 18 (Balaji Raghavan vs. Union of India, 1996). They recognize merit.
*   No citizen of India shall accept any title from any foreign State.
    *   Example cited: Sunil Gavaskar and potential knighthood.
    *   Violation of Article 18 is not currently punishable by any specific law.

##### ❓ 🕒 [54:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=3261s) **PYQ (Prelims 2020) Example**
*   *Question*: Which one of the following categories of fundamental rights incorporates protection against untouchability as a form of discrimination?
*   *Answer*: (d) Right to equality (since Article 17 falls under Articles 14-18).

#### 🕊️ 🕒 [55:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=3341s) **6.4 Right to Freedom (Articles 19-22)**

##### 🗣️ 🕒 [55:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=3355s) **6.4.1 Article 19: Protection of certain rights regarding freedom of speech, etc.**
*   Available **only to citizens**.
*   `**Article 19 (1)**` guarantees six freedoms (originally seven, right to property omitted):
    *   **(a) Freedom of speech and expression.**
        *   Reasonable restrictions (`Clause 2`): sovereignty and integrity of India, security of the State, friendly relations with foreign States, public order, decency or morality, contempt of court, defamation, incitement to an offence. (8 restrictions).
        *   *Original restrictions*: security of state, decency/morality, contempt of court, defamation.
        *   *Added later*: sovereignty/integrity, friendly relations, public order, incitement. (Public order added by 1 st Amendment, 1951).
        *   Discussion on obscenity vs. Disgust.
    *   **(b) To assemble peaceably and without arms.**
        *   Reasonable restrictions (`Clause 3`): sovereignty and integrity of India, public order.
    *   **(c) To form associations or unions or co-operative societies.**
        *   "Co-operative societies" added by 97 th Amendment, 2011.
        *   Reasonable restrictions (`Clause 4`): sovereignty and integrity of India, public order, morality.
    *   **(d) To move freely throughout the territory of India.**
    *   **(e) To reside and settle in any part of the territory of India.**
        *   (d) & (e) promote **Fraternity**.
        *   Reasonable restrictions (`Clause 5`) for (d) & (e): interests of the general public, protection of the interests of any Scheduled Tribe (e.g., restricting movement in tribal areas like Jarawas, Sentinels; restricting movement during COVID).
    *   **(f) Omitted** (Right to acquire, hold and dispose of property) by 44 th Amendment, 1978.
    *   **(g) To practise any profession, or to carry on any occupation, trade or business.**
        *   Reasonable restrictions (`Clause 6`): interests of the general public.
        *   State can prescribe **professional or technical qualifications**.
        *   State can create **state monopolies** (partial or complete exclusion of citizens from any trade, business, industry or service). E.g., atomic energy.

##### 🔗 🕒 [01:04:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=3865s) **6.4.2 Article 20: Protection in respect of conviction for offences**
*   Provides three types of protection:
    1.  `**Clause (1)**`: **No ex-post-facto criminal legislation.**
        *   A person can only be convicted for an act that was an offence under the law in force *at the time of the commission of the act*.
        *   Penalty imposed cannot be greater than what might have been inflicted under the law in force at the time of the commission of the offence.
        *   Criminal laws cannot have retrospective effect; civil laws can.
    2.  `**Clause (2)**`: **No double jeopardy.**
        *   No person shall be prosecuted and punished for the same offence more than once.
    3.  `**Clause (3)**`: **No self-incrimination.**
        *   No person accused of any offence shall be compelled to be a witness against himself.
        *   Tests like Narco analysis, polygraph, brain mapping, lie detector tests are unconstitutional (violate Art 20 (3) and Right to Privacy).
        *   DNA fingerprinting is *not* considered a violation of Article 20 (3).

##### ❤️ 🕒 [01:07:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=4064s) **6.4.3 Article 21: Protection of life and personal liberty**
*   "No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to **procedure established by law**."
*   In **Maneka Gandhi case (1978)**, Supreme Court interpreted "procedure established by law" to include elements of "due process of law" and "principles of natural justice."
*   Right to life has been expanded to include many rights like right to livelihood, clean environment, privacy, speedy trial, etc.

##### 🎓 🕒 [01:09:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=4156s) **6.4.4 Article 21 A: Right to education**
*   Added by **86 th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002**.
*   "The State shall provide free and compulsory education to all children of the age of **six to fourteen years** in such manner as the State may, by law, determine."
*   This is a Fundamental Right.

##### 👮 🕒 [01:10:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=4206s) **6.4.5 Article 22: Protection against arrest and detention in certain cases**
*   Provides safeguards for persons arrested or detained.
*   Not available to:
    *   **Enemy aliens**.
    *   Persons arrested or detained under **preventive detention laws**.
*   **Rights of an arrested person (under ordinary law)**:
    1.  To be **informed of the grounds of arrest** (in written format, as per SC).
    2.  To **consult and be defended by a legal practitioner** of his choice.
    3.  To be **produced before the nearest magistrate within 24 hours** of arrest (excluding journey time).
    4.  Cannot be detained beyond 24 hours without the authority of a magistrate.
*   **Preventive Detention**:
    *   Maximum detention without trial by an advisory board: **3 months**.
    *   Detention beyond 3 months requires approval of an Advisory Board.

#### ⚒️ 🕒 [01:13:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=4384s) **6.5 Right against Exploitation (Articles 23-24)**

##### 🚫🚶‍♂️ 🕒 [01:13:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=4403s) **6.5.1 Article 23: Prohibition of traffic in human beings and forced labour**
*   Prohibits:
    *   **Traffic in human beings**: Buying and selling of men, women, and children like goods.
    *   **Begar**: Forced labour without payment.
    *   Other similar forms of **forced labour**.
*   Slavery, though not explicitly mentioned, is covered (Dina Nath v. Union of India / Deena @ Deena v. Union of India, likely referring to Bandhua Mukti Morcha or similar forced labor cases).
*   **Exception**: State can impose **compulsory service for public purposes** (e.g., military service, social service during calamities).
    *   In imposing such service, the State **shall not make any discrimination** on grounds *only* of religion, race, caste or class or any of them. (Discrimination on grounds like age, sex, residence may be permissible).

##### 🧒 🕒 [01:16:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=4585s) **6.5.2 Article 24: Prohibition of employment of children in factories, etc.**
*   Prohibits employment of children **below the age of 14 years** in any factory, mine, or other hazardous employment.
    *   "Child" for this article means below 14 years.
*   Parliament enacted Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act, 1986 (amended in 2016, which also addresses adolescents 14-18).

##### ❓ 🕒 [01:18:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=4722s) **PYQ (Prelims 2017) Example**
*   *Question*: Which of the following are envisaged by the Right against Exploitation in the Constitution of India?
    1.  Prohibition of traffic in human beings and forced labour.
    2.  Abolition of untouchability.
    3.  Protection of the interests of minorities.
    4.  Prohibition of employment of children in factories and mines.

#### 🙏 🕒 [01:19:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=4787s) **6.6 Right to Freedom of Religion (Articles 25-28)**

##### 🕊️ 🕒 [01:20:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=4805s) **6.6.1 Article 25: Freedom of conscience and free profession, practice and propagation of religion**
*   Grants to **all persons** (citizens and non-citizens):
    *   Freedom of conscience.
    *   Right to freely profess, practice, and propagate religion. (Propagation does not include forced conversion).
*   **Restrictions**: This right is subject to:
    1.  **Public order, morality, and health.**
    2.  **Other provisions of Part III (Fundamental Rights)**.
        *   E.g., Sabarimala temple case: Religious freedom cannot violate Article 14 (equality) or Article 17 (untouchability, in a broader sense of discriminatory exclusion).
*   **State's power to regulate/restrict**:
    *   Economic, financial, political, or other secular activities associated with religious practice.
    *   Social welfare and reform, including throwing open Hindu religious institutions of a public character to all classes and sections of Hindus.
*   `**Explanation I**`: Wearing and carrying of **kirpans** shall be deemed to be included in the profession of the Sikh religion.
*   `**Explanation II**`: Reference to "Hindus" shall be construed as including persons professing the **Sikh, Jaina, or Buddhist** religion.

##### ⛪ 🕒 [01:23:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=5011s) **6.6.2 Article 26: Freedom to manage religious affairs**
*   Subject to public order, morality, and health, every **religious denomination** or any section thereof shall have the right:
    *   To establish and maintain institutions for religious and charitable purposes.
    *   To manage its own affairs in matters of religion.
    *   To own and acquire movable and immovable property.
    *   To administer such property in accordance with law.
*   This right is for collective religious freedom (denominations, e.g., Shia/Sunni Muslims, various Hindu sects).
*   Kesavananda Bharati case was filed invoking Article 26.

##### 💸 🕒 [01:25:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=5119s) **6.6.3 Article 27: Freedom as to payment of taxes for promotion of any particular religion**
*   No person shall be compelled to pay any **taxes**, the proceeds of which are specifically appropriated in payment of expenses for the promotion or maintenance of any particular religion or religious denomination.
*   This reinforces India's secular character (prohibits religious taxes like Jaziya).
*   *Fees* for secular administration of religious institutions are permissible, but not *taxes* for religious promotion.

##### 🏫 🕒 [01:25:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=5159s) **6.6.4 Article 28: Freedom as to attendance at religious instruction or religious worship in certain educational institutions**
*   `**Clause (1)**`: No religious instruction shall be provided in any educational institution **wholly maintained out of State funds**.
*   `**Clause (2)**`: Clause (1) does not apply to an educational institution administered by the State but established under any **endowment or trust** which requires that religious instruction shall be imparted in such institution (e.g., Madrasas, Gurukuls, Pathshalas, Convent schools set up by trusts with such mandate).
*   `**Clause (3)**`: No person attending any educational institution **recognized by the State or receiving aid out of State funds** shall be required to take part in any religious instruction or attend any religious worship without their consent (or guardian's consent if a minor).
    *   Relevant to discussions like Aligarh Muslim University (AMU) minority status. (Though AMU case is more about Article 30).

#### 🌍 🕒 [01:30:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=5434s) **6.7 Cultural and Educational Rights (Articles 29-30)**
*   These rights are exclusively available to **citizens**.

##### 🗣️📜 🕒 [01:30:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=5457s) **6.7.1 Article 29: Protection of interests of minorities**
*   `**Clause (1)**`: Any **section of the citizens** residing in the territory of India or any part thereof having a **distinct language, script or culture** of its own shall have the right to **conserve** the same.
    *   Term "minority" is not defined in the Constitution.
    *   This right is available to *any section of citizens*, not just minorities, though often invoked by minorities.
    *   Promotes "unity in diversity."
    *   Right to conserve language includes the right to agitate for its protection (SC interpretation).
*   `**Clause (2)**`: No citizen shall be denied admission into any educational institution **maintained by the State or receiving aid out of State funds** on grounds *only* of religion, race, caste, language or any of them.
    *   Discrimination on other grounds (e.g., merit, qualifications) may be permissible.

##### 🎓🤝 🕒 [01:33:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=5604s) **6.7.2 Article 30: Right of minorities to establish and administer educational institutions**
*   `**Clause (1)**`: All **minorities, whether based on religion or language**, shall have the right to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice.
    *   Recognizes two types of minorities: **religious** and **linguistic**.
*   `**Clause (1 A)**`: (Added by 44 th Amendment) In making any law providing for the compulsory acquisition of any property of an educational institution established and administered by a minority, the State shall ensure that the amount fixed by or determined under such law for the acquisition of such property is such as would not restrict or abrogate the right guaranteed under that clause.
*   `**Clause (2)**`: The State shall not, in granting aid to educational institutions, **discriminate** against any educational institution on the ground that it is under the management of a minority, whether based on religion or language.
*   Minority educational institutions are exempt from reservations for SC/ST/OBC/EWS.
    *   They can reserve up to 50% of seats for their own community members.
    *   This is why communities like Lingayats in Karnataka demand minority status.

#### 💾 🕒 [01:35:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=5754s) **6.8 Saving of Certain Laws (Articles 31 A, 31 B, 31 C)**
*   Article 31 (Right to Property) was repealed as a Fundamental Right by 44 th Amendment, 1978. It's now a legal right under Article 300 A.

##### 🏞️ 🕒 [01:36:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=5773s) **6.8.1 Article 31 A: Saving of laws providing for acquisition of estates, etc.**
*   Protects laws related to agrarian reform and acquisition of estates/property by the State from challenge on grounds of violation of Article 14 (equality) and Article 19 (freedoms). (Originally also Article 31).
*   Such laws made by a State Legislature must have been reserved for the President's consideration and received his assent to get this protection.
*   Allows for:
    *   Acquisition of estates and related rights.
    *   Taking over management of property.
    *   Amalgamation of corporations.
    *   Extinguishment or modification of rights of managing agents, secretaries, treasurers, managing directors, directors, or managers of corporations, or of any voting rights of shareholders thereof.
    *   Extinguishment or modification of mining leases.

##### 🛡️📅 🕒 [01:38:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=5898s) **6.8.2 Article 31 B: Validation of certain Acts and Regulations (Ninth Schedule)**
*   Introduced by the **1 st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1951**, along with the **Ninth Schedule**.
*   States that none of the Acts and Regulations specified in the Ninth Schedule shall be deemed to be void, or ever to have become void, on the ground that they are inconsistent with any Fundamental Rights.
*   Provides a "retrospective effect" of validation.
*   **Judicial Review of Ninth Schedule Laws**:
    *   **I.R. Coelho case (2007)**:
        *   Laws placed in the Ninth Schedule *before* **April 24, 1973** (date of Kesavananda Bharati judgment) are immune from challenge.
        *   Laws placed in the Ninth Schedule *on or after* **April 24, 1973** can be challenged if they violate the Basic Structure of the Constitution (which includes FRs that form part of the basic structure).
*   ❓ **PYQ (Prelims 2019) Example**:
    *   *Question*: The Ninth Schedule was introduced in the Constitution of India during the Prime Ministership of?
    *   *Answer*: Jawaharlal Nehru (as 1 st Amendment was in 1951).

##### 🎯 🕒 [01:41:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=6099s) **6.8.3 Article 31 C: Saving of laws giving effect to certain directive principles**
*   Introduced by **25 th Amendment Act, 1971**:
    *   Stated that no law giving effect to policy of the State towards securing principles specified in **Article 39 (b)** (equitable distribution of material resources) and **Article 39 (c)** (prevention of concentration of wealth) shall be void on the ground that it is inconsistent with, or takes away or abridges any of the rights conferred by Article 14, Article 19 (or Article 31 - now repealed).
*   Amended by **42 nd Amendment Act, 1976**:
    *   Extended protection to laws implementing **any or all Directive Principles (Part IV)**, not just 39 (b) & (c).
    *   Also added that no law containing a declaration that it is for giving effect to such policy shall be called in question in any court on the ground that it does not give effect to such policy (ousting judicial review on this aspect).
*   **Minerva Mills case (1980)**:
    *   The Supreme Court struck down the expansion of Article 31 C to *all* DPSPs by the 42 nd Amendment.
    *   Held that this disturbed the balance between FRs and DPSPs, which is part of the Basic Structure.
    *   The part ousting judicial review was also struck down.
*   **Current Status of Article 31 C**:
    *   Only laws implementing **Article 39 (b) and 39 (c)** have primacy over Articles 14 and 19.
    *   The text of the Constitution still shows the 42 nd Amendment version, but its interpretation is as per Minerva Mills.

#### 🏛️🔨 🕒 [01:47:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=6451s) **6.9 Article 32: Remedies for enforcement of rights conferred by this Part (Right to Constitutional Remedies)**
*   Dr. Ambedkar called it the "fundamental of all fundamental rights" or "heart and soul" of the Constitution.
*   Guarantees the right to move the **Supreme Court** for the enforcement of FRs.
*   The Supreme Court shall have power to issue directions or orders or **writs**, including writs in the nature of:
    1.  **Habeas Corpus** (to produce the body)
    2.  **Mandamus** (we command)
    3.  **Prohibition** (to forbid)
    4.  **Certiorari** (to be certified/informed)
    5.  **Quo Warranto** (by what authority)
*   This right itself is a Fundamental Right.
*   `**Clause (3)**`: Parliament may by law empower any **other court** to exercise within the local limits of its jurisdiction all or any of the powers exercisable by the Supreme Court under clause (2). (This power has not been used yet).
*   `**Clause (4)**`: This right shall not be suspended except as otherwise provided for by the Constitution (e.g., during National Emergency, but even then, enforcement of Art 20 & 21 cannot be suspended).

#### ⚔️ 🕒 [01:50:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=6630s) **6.10 Article 33: Power of Parliament to modify the rights conferred by this Part in their application to Forces, etc.**
*   Parliament may by law restrict or abrogate FRs for:
    *   Members of the **Armed Forces**.
    *   Members of the Forces charged with the **maintenance of public order** (e.g., police, CRPF).
    *   Persons employed in any **intelligence or counter-intelligence organisation** (e.g., IB, RAW).
    *   Persons employed in, or in connection with, the **telecommunication systems** set up for the purposes of any Force, bureau or organisation referred to in clauses (a) to (c).
*   This power is with **Parliament only**, not State Legislatures.
*   Purpose: To ensure proper discharge of their duties and maintenance of discipline among them.

#### 📜🛡️ 🕒 [01:52:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=6740s) **6.11 Article 34: Restriction on rights conferred by this Part while martial law is in force in any area**
*   Term **"martial law" is not defined** in the Constitution.
*   Parliament may by law indemnify any person in the service of the Union or a State or any other person for any act done by them in connection with the maintenance or restoration of order in any area where martial law was in force.
*   Validates sentences passed, punishments inflicted, forfeitures ordered or other acts done under martial law.
*   This power is with **Parliament only**.

#### 📝 🕒 [01:53:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=6829s) **6.12 Article 35: Legislation to give effect to the provisions of this Part**
*   Grants **exclusive power to Parliament** (and not State Legislatures) to make laws with respect to:
    *   `Article 16 (3)`: Prescribing residence as a condition for certain employments.
    *   `Article 32 (3)`: Empowering courts other than SC/HC to issue writs.
    *   `Article 33`: Modifying FRs for armed forces, etc.
    *   `Article 34`: Indemnifying acts done during martial law.
*   Also, Parliament (and not State Legislatures) has exclusive power to make laws for **prescribing punishment for those acts which are declared to be offences under Part III** (e.g., untouchability under Art 17, forced labour under Art 23).

### 📜✍️ 🕒 [01:56:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=7000s) **7.0 Part IV: Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP) (Articles 36-51)**
*   Principles that direct the State when making policies and enacting laws.

#### 🏢 🕒 [01:57:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=7029s) **7.1 Article 36: Definition of "State"**
*   For Part IV, "State" has the **same meaning as in Part III (Article 12)**, unless the context otherwise requires.
*   DPSPs are **positive obligations** on the State (asking the State to interfere for welfare), unlike FRs which are mostly negative obligations (asking State to refrain).

#### ✅ 🕒 [01:58:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=7111s) **7.2 Article 37: Application of the principles contained in this Part**
*   Two important characteristics of DPSPs:
    1.  They shall **not be enforceable by any court**.
    2.  Nevertheless, they are **fundamental in the governance of the country**, and it shall be the **duty of the State to apply these principles** in making laws.
*   They aim to establish a **welfare state** and promote **socio-economic democracy**.
*   BN Rau (Constitutional Advisor) wished they were justiciable.
*   Their non-justiciability doesn't make them inferior; State needs time and resources to implement them.

##### ❓ 🕒 [02:00:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=7202s) **PYQ Examples on Article 37 & DPSPs**
*   **Prelims 2020**: *With reference to provisions in Part IV... Which are correct?*

    *   (They shall not be enforceable... But principles laid down influence making of laws - Correct)

*   **Prelims (Year not specified)**: *According to Constitution... Which are fundamental for governance?*

#### 🌟 🕒 [02:02:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=7347s) **7.3 Article 39: Certain principles of policy to be followed by the State**
*   The State shall, in particular, direct its policy towards securing:
    *   **(b)** that the **ownership and control of the material resources of the community** are so distributed as best to subserve the common good.
        *   **Bombay Private Property Owners Association case (2024)**: SC clarified that "material resource of a community" does not include *all* private property. Earlier, Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer and Justice Chinnappa Reddy had interpreted it to include private property.
    *   **(c)** that the operation of the economic system does not result in the **concentration of wealth** and means of production to the common detriment.
*   ❓ **PYQ (Prelims 2021)**: *Under Indian Constitution, concentration of wealth violates?*
    *   *Answer*: Directive Principles of State Policy.

#### ➕📜 🕒 [02:05:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=7516s) **7.4 DPSPs Added by Amendments**
*   **Added by 42 nd Amendment Act, 1976**:
    1.  `**Article 39 A**`: Equal justice and free legal aid.
    2.  `**Article 43 A**`: Participation of workers in the management of industries. (PYQ mentioned)
    3.  `**Article 48 A**`: Protection and improvement of environment and safeguarding of forests and wildlife.
*   **Added by 97 th Amendment Act, 2011**:
    1.  `**Article 43 B**`: Promotion of co-operative societies.
*   **Modified by 86 th Amendment Act, 2002**:
    1.  `**Article 45**`: Provision for early childhood care and education to children **below the age of six years**.
        *   Original Article 45 provided for education for children 6-14 years, which became FR under Art 21 A.

*   `**Article 40**`: Organisation of village panchayats.
*   `**Article 44**`: Uniform civil code for the citizens.
*   `**Article 49**`: Protection of monuments and places and objects of national importance.
*   `**Article 50**`: Separation of judiciary from executive.
*   `**Article 51**`: Promotion of international peace and security.

    *   **(c) U**phold and protect the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India. (`ACU - U`)
    *   **(d) D**efend the country and render national service when called upon to do so. (`DVD - D`)
    *   **(e) P**romote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood amongst all the people of India... Renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women. (`PR - P`)
    *   **(f) V**alue and preserve the rich heritage of our composite culture. (`DVD - V`)
    *   **(g) P**rotect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers and wild life, and to have compassion for living creatures. (`SSP - P`)
    *   **(h) D**evelop the scientific temper, humanism and the spirit of inquiry and reform. (`DVD - D`)
    *   **(i) S**afeguard public property and to abjure violence. (`SSP - S`)
    *   **(j) S**trive towards excellence in all spheres of individual and collective activity... (`SSP - S`)

### 👑 🕒 [02:16:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=8171s) **9.0 Part V: The Union (Articles 52 onwards)**
*   Part V deals with The Union.

#### 👤 🕒 [02:17:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=8244s) **9.1 The President (Articles 52-62)**

##### 🇮🇳👑 🕒 [02:17:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=8244s) **9.1.1 Article 52: The President of India**
*   "There **shall be** a President of India." (Shortest article).
*   Implication of "shall": Post must always be occupied; cannot remain vacant.

##### 🛡️ 🕒 [02:17:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=8267s) **9.1.2 Article 53: Executive power of the Union**
*   **(1)** Executive power of the Union shall be **vested in the President** and exercised by him directly or through subordinate officers.
*   **(2)** Supreme command of the **Defence Forces** of the Union shall be vested in the President.
*   **(3)** This article does not:
    *   Transfer to the President any functions conferred by existing law on the government of any State or other authority.
    *   Prevent Parliament from **conferring by law functions on authorities other than the President**. (Important point).

##### 🗳️ 🕒 [02:19:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=8393s) **9.1.3 Article 54: Election of President**
*   Elected by members of an **Electoral College** consisting of:
    *   **(a)** Elected members of both Houses of Parliament (MPs).
    *   **(b)** Elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the States (MLAs).
*   `**Explanation**`: "State" includes the National Capital Territory of Delhi and the Union Territory of Puducherry (added by 70 th Amendment Act, 1992).
    *   J&K MLAs are currently *not* part of the Electoral College; requires a constitutional amendment ratified by states.

##### 📊 🕒 [02:22:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=8557s) **9.1.4 Article 55: Manner of election of President**
*   Election by system of **proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote** and voting shall be by **secret ballot**.
*   Ensures uniformity in the scale of representation of different States and parity between States as a whole and the Union.
*   **Value of vote of an MLA**: `(Total population of State) / (Total number of elected MLAs of State) * (1/1000)`
*   **Value of vote of an MP**: `(Total value of votes of all elected MLAs of all States) / (Total number of elected MPs)`
*   Population figures used are based on the **1971 census** (until the first census after 2026 data is published). This is mentioned in Article 55.

##### ⏳ 🕒 [02:24:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=8670s) **9.1.5 Article 56: Term of office of President**
*   **(1)** Term: **5 years** from the date on which he enters upon his office.
*   **Provisos**:
    *   **(a)** May resign by writing to the **Vice-President**.
    *   **(b)** May be removed by **impeachment** for **violation of the Constitution** (procedure in Art 61).
        *   Article 56 mentions the *ground* for impeachment. Article 61 details the *procedure*.
    *   **(c)** Shall continue to hold office, notwithstanding expiration of term, until his successor enters upon office (prevents interregnum).
*   **(2)** Resignation communicated by Vice-President to the **Speaker of the Lok Sabha**.

##### 🔄🗳️ 🕒 [02:29:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=8952s) **9.1.6 Article 57: Eligibility for re-election**
*   A person who holds, or who has held, office as President **shall be eligible for re-election** to that office.
*   No limit on the number of terms (unlike US President - two terms).

##### ✅ 🕒 [02:30:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=9002s) **9.1.7 Article 58: Qualifications for election as President**
*   **(1)** No person shall be eligible unless he:
    *   Is a **citizen of India**.
    *   Has completed **35 years of age**.
    *   Is **qualified for election as a member of the House of the People (Lok Sabha)**.
*   **(2)** Shall not hold any **office of profit** under the Government of India or any State Government or local/other authority.
*   `**Explanation**`: President, Vice-President, Governor, or Minister (Union or State) are **not deemed to hold an office of profit** for this purpose. They can contest while holding these offices.

##### 📜 🕒 [02:31:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=9081s) **9.1.8 Article 59: Conditions of President's office**
*   **(1)** President shall not be a member of either House of Parliament or of a State Legislature.
    *   If such a member is elected President, he shall be **deemed to have vacated his seat** in that House on the date he enters upon his office as President.
*   **(2)** Shall not hold any other office of profit.
*   **(3)** Entitled to official residences, emoluments, allowances, privileges as determined by Parliament.
*   **(4)** Emoluments and allowances shall not be diminished during his term.

##### 🤝 🕒 [02:33:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=9215s) **9.1.9 Article 60: Oath or affirmation by the President**
*   Oath is mentioned in the Article itself (not in Third Schedule like many others).
*   Administered by the **Chief Justice of India (CJI)** or, in his absence, the **senior-most Judge of the Supreme Court available**.
*   President takes oath to "preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law."

##### ⚖️ 🕒 [02:34:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=9282s) **9.1.10 Article 61: Procedure for impeachment of the President**
*   **(1)** Ground: **Violation of the Constitution**.
*   **(2)** Charge can be preferred by **either House of Parliament**.
    *   Requires a notice in writing signed by not less than **one-fourth of the total members** of that House.
    *   Resolution moved after at least **14 days' notice**.
    *   Resolution must be passed by a majority of **not less than two-thirds of the total membership** of that House.
*   **(3)** Other House investigates or causes the charge to be investigated. President has the right to appear and be represented.
*   **(4)** If investigating House also passes resolution by **two-thirds of its total membership** declaring charge sustained, President stands removed from date of resolution.

##### 🕰️ 🕒 [02:34:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=9299s) **9.1.11 Article 62: Time of holding election to fill vacancy in the office of President and the term of office of person elected to fill casual vacancy**
*   **(1)** Election to fill vacancy caused by expiration of term must be completed **before the expiration of the term**.
*   **(2)** Election to fill vacancy due to **death, resignation, or removal** (casual vacancy) must be held **as soon as possible and in no case later than six months** from date of vacancy.
    *   Person elected to fill a casual vacancy holds office for a **full term of 5 years**. (Unlike MPs/MLAs who fill remainder of term).

#### 🥈 🕒 [02:39:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=9545s) **9.2 The Vice-President (Articles 63-71)**

##### 🇮🇳🥈 🕒 [02:39:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=9545s) **9.2.1 Article 63: The Vice-President of India**
*   "There **shall be** a Vice-President of India."

##### 🏛️👨‍⚖️ 🕒 [02:39:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=9574s) **9.2.2 Article 64: The Vice-President to be ex-officio Chairman of the Council of States**
*   Vice-President shall be **ex-officio 
Chairman of the Council of States (Rajya Sabha)**.
*   Shall not hold any other office of profit.
*   Proviso: When VP acts as President (Art 65), he shall not perform duties of Chairman of Rajya Sabha and is not entitled to salary/allowances of Chairman.

##### 🚶‍♂️➡️👑 🕒 [02:40:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=9631s) **9.2.3 Article 65: The Vice-President to act as President or to discharge his functions during casual vacancies in the office, or during the absence, of President**
*   **(1)** In case of vacancy in President's office (death, resignation, removal), **VP shall act as President** until a new President is elected (within 6 months) and enters office.
*   **(2)** When President is unable to discharge functions due to **absence, illness or any other cause**, VP shall discharge functions until President resumes duties.
    *   Examples: Dr. Rajendra Prasad's illness and foreign visit, where VP acted as President.
*   **(3)** When VP acts as President or discharges his functions, he shall have all powers and immunities of the President and be entitled to emoluments, allowances, and privileges as determined by Parliament for the President.

##### 🗳️🥈 🕒 [02:43:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=9793s) **9.2.4 Article 66: Election of Vice-President**
*   **(1)** Elected by members of an **Electoral College** consisting of:
    *   Members of **both Houses of Parliament** (elected and nominated).
*   Election by system of **proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote** and voting by **secret ballot**.
*   **(2)** VP shall not be a member of Parliament or State Legislature. If such member elected VP, deemed to have vacated seat on entering office as VP.
*   **(3)** Qualifications:
    *   Citizen of India.
    *   Completed 35 years of age.
    *   Qualified for election as a member of the **Council of States (Rajya Sabha)**.
*   **(4)** Shall not hold any office of profit. (Similar explanation as for President regarding not deeming current high offices as office of profit for contestation).

##### ⏳🥈 🕒 [02:44:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=9850s) **9.2.5 Article 67: Term of office of Vice-President**
*   Term: **5 years** from the date on which he enters upon his office.
*   **Provisos**:
    *   **(a)** May resign by writing to the **President**.
    *   **(b)** May be **removed** by a resolution of the **Council of States (Rajya Sabha)** passed by a majority of all the then members of the Council (effective majority) and agreed to by the **House of the People (Lok Sabha)** (simple majority).
        *   Requires at least **14 days' notice** for moving the resolution.
        *   **No specific grounds for removal** are mentioned in the Constitution for the VP (unlike "violation of Constitution" for President).
    *   **(c)** Shall continue to hold office, notwithstanding expiration of term, until his successor enters upon office.

##### 🕰️🥈 🕒 [02:47:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=10021s) **9.2.6 Article 68: Time of holding election to fill vacancy in the office of Vice-President and the term of office of person elected to fill casual vacancy**
*   **(1)** Election to fill vacancy caused by expiration of term must be completed **before the expiration of the term**.
*   **(2)** Election to fill vacancy due to **death, resignation, or removal** (casual vacancy) must be held **as soon as possible** after the occurrence of the vacancy.
    *   **Important Distinction**: Unlike President (max 6 months), there's **no "not later than 6 months"** clause for VP's casual vacancy election.
    *   Person elected to fill a casual vacancy holds office for a **full term of 5 years**.

##### 🤝🥈 🕒 [02:49:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=10156s) **9.2.7 Article 69: Oath or affirmation by the Vice-President**
*   Oath is mentioned in the Article itself (not in Third Schedule).
*   Administered by the **President** or some person appointed in that behalf by him.
*   VP takes oath to "bear true faith and allegiance to the Constitution of India as by law established" and to "faithfully discharge the duty upon which I am about to enter."
    *   Difference from President's oath: VP does not swear to "preserve, protect and defend the Constitution."

##### 🤔👑 🕒 [02:50:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=10222s) **9.2.8 Article 70: Discharge of President's functions in other contingencies**
*   **Parliament may make such provision as it thinks fit** for the discharge of the functions of the President in any contingency **not provided for in this Chapter (Chapter I of Part V)**.
*   Example: If both President and Vice-President offices are vacant simultaneously.
    *   Constitution is silent on this specific contingency.
    *   Parliament enacted the **President (Discharge of Functions) Act, 1969**.
    *   This Act provides the order of succession:
        1.  President
        2.  Vice-President
        3.  Chief Justice of India
        4.  Senior-most Judge of the Supreme Court.
    *   This succession beyond VP is based on a Parliamentary law, not directly in the Constitution.

##### ⚖️👑🥈 🕒 [02:53:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=10414s) **9.2.9 Article 71: Matters relating to, or connected with, the election of a President or Vice-President**
*   **(1)** All doubts and disputes arising out of or in connection with the election of President or VP shall be inquired into and decided by the **Supreme Court**, whose decision shall be final.
*   **(2)** If Supreme Court declares election void, acts done by President/VP prior to declaration are **not invalidated**.
*   **(3)** Parliament may by law regulate any matter relating to or connected with the election.
*   **(4)** Election of President or VP **shall not be called in question on the ground of the existence of any vacancy** for whatever reason among the members of the electoral college electing him.
    *   E.g., If a State Assembly is dissolved, or some MP/MLA seats are vacant.

#### 🙏👑 🕒 [02:55:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VRriZo5WCRE&t=10503s) **9.3 Article 72: Power of President to grant pardons, etc., and to suspend, remit or commute sentences in certain cases (Pardoning Power)**
*   President shall have the power to grant **pardons, reprieves, respites or remissions of punishment or to suspend, remit or commute the sentence** of any person convicted of any offence:
    *   **(a)** In all cases where the punishment or sentence is by a **Court Martial**;
    *   **(b)** In all cases where the punishment or sentence is for an offence against any law relating to a matter to which the **executive power of the Union extends**;
    *   **(c)** In all cases where the sentence is a **sentence of death**.
*   This power is exercised on the **advice of the Council of Ministers (government)**; it is not a discretionary power.
*   Governor also has pardoning power (Article 161), with some distinctions (e.g., death sentence, court martial), to be discussed later.

### 🏛️ 🕒 [01:30](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=90) **1.0 The Union Executive (Continued)**

#### 📜 🕒 [01:30](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=90) **1.1 Article 74: Council of Ministers to Aid and Advise President**
*   Reads Article 52 (President of India) and Article 53 (Executive powers vested in President) with Article 74.
*   **Original Provision (26 th January 1950):**
    *   "There shall be a **Council of Ministers** with the **Prime Minister** at the head to **aid and advise the President**." (`[02:18]`)
*   **Amendments to Article 74:**
    1.  📜 **42 nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976** (Shrimati Indira Gandhi as PM):
        *   Added: The President "**who shall act on that advice**". (`[02:52]`)
        *   This made the advice of the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers **binding** on the President.
    2.  📜 **44 th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978** (Janata Party government):
        *   Amended further: The President can ask for **reconsideration** of the advice. (`[03:29]`)
        *   However, if the same advice is given again after reconsideration, the President **must act** on that advice.
*   **Combined Effect of Amendments:** (`[03:55]`)
    *   There shall be a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head to aid and advise the President.
    *   The President *shall* act on that advice.
    *   The President *may* ask for reconsideration of that advice.
    *   Advice given after reconsideration *shall be binding* on the President.
    *   *Example:* If PM & CoM advise promulgating an ordinance, President can ask for reconsideration, but if reiterated, President must accept. (`[04:19]`)
*   **Article 74 Clause 2:** (`[04:41]`)
    *   The advice tendered by Ministers to the President **shall not be inquired into any court of law**.
    *   This implies **no judicial review** on this specific matter.

#### 📜 🕒 [04:54](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=294) **1.2 Article 75: Other Provisions as to Ministers**
*   **Appointment of Prime Minister:**
    *   The Prime Minister **shall be appointed by the President**. (`[05:06]`)
    *   Constitution does not specify who this PM has to be.
    *   President enjoys **situational discretion**: (`[05:21]`)
        *   No discretion if a political party has a clear majority (leader of that party is appointed).
        *   Discretion arises in a **hung Parliament** (no party with a clear majority); President can appoint anyone as PM.
*   **Appointment of Other Ministers:**
    *   Other ministers **shall be appointed by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister**. (`[06:11]`)
    *   This is **binding** on the President.
    *   No discretion for the President in appointing other ministers.
*   **Strength of Council of Ministers:**
    *   Constitution does not classify ministers (e.g., Cabinet Minister, Minister of State). It only mentions PM and Council of Ministers. (`[06:46]`)
    *   **Originally no limit** on the strength.
    *   📜 **91 st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003** (Mr. Vajpayee as PM): (`[07:10]`)
        *   Imposed a limit: The strength of the Council of Ministers, **including the Prime Minister**, shall **not exceed 15% of the total number of members of the Lok Sabha**.
*   **Ministerial Appointment of Disqualified Members (Defection):** (`[07:41]`)
    *   A legislator can be disqualified on grounds of defection under the **10 th Schedule**.
    *   Disqualifying authority: Speaker of Lok Sabha/Legislative Assembly, Chairperson of Rajya Sabha/Legislative Council.
    *   A **defector cannot be appointed as a minister**. (`[08:06]`)
    *   This bar continues **unless and until they get re-elected** to Parliament.
    *   This provision is mentioned in Article 75.
*   **Tenure of Ministers:**
    *   Ministers **shall hold office during the pleasure of the President**. (`[08:37]`)
    *   In reality, this means the **pleasure of the Prime Minister**.
    *   No defined tenure (not necessarily 5 years).
*   **Collective Responsibility:** (`[09:14]`)
    *   The Council of Ministers **shall be collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha** (Lower House).
    *   This is a cornerstone of **parliamentary democracy**.
    *   *UPSC Prelims relevance:* This has been asked multiple times.
*   **Oath of Office and Secrecy:** (`[09:53]`)
    *   Administered by the **President** before a minister assumes office.
    *   The form of oath/affirmation is mentioned in the **Third Schedule** of the Constitution.
*   **Appointment of a Non-Member as Minister:** (`[10:21]`)
    *   A person who is **not a member of either House of Parliament** can be appointed as a minister.
    *   However, they must become a member of either Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha **within six months**.
    *   *Example:* Dr. S. Jaishankar was not an MP when sworn in in 2019; later elected to Rajya Sabha. (`[11:00]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [11:25](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=685) **1.3 Article 76: Attorney General for India**
*   **Appointment:** Appointed by the **President**. (`[11:25]`)
*   **Qualification:** (`[11:38]`)
    *   Must be a person **qualified to be appointed as a Judge of the Supreme Court**.
    *   This does *not* mean they must be a current Supreme Court judge (due to separation of powers). (`[12:01]`)
    *   **Qualifications for a Supreme Court Judge:** (`[12:24]`)
        1.  Citizen of India.
        2.  AND (any of the following):
            *   Judge of one or more High Courts for **5 years**.
            *   Advocate in one or more High Courts for **10 years**.
            *   A **distinguished jurist** in the opinion of the President.
*   **Role and Functions:** (`[13:21]`)
    *   Represents the **Union Government** in all cases where it is a party.
    *   Gives **legal advice** to the Government of India upon such legal matters referred by the President.
    *   Performs other duties of a legal character assigned by the President.
    *   The **President can enlarge the role** of the Attorney General (not Parliament, PM, or CJI). (`[14:11]`)
*   **Rights:**
    *   **Right of audience in all courts** in the territory of India. (`[14:24]`)
*   **Tenure:**
    *   **Does not enjoy security of tenure**. Tenure is not mentioned in the Constitution. (`[14:51]`)
    *   Holds office **during the pleasure of the President** (effectively, pleasure of the PM).
*   **Remuneration:**
    *   Determined by the **President** (not Parliament). (`[15:05]`)
*   **Rights in Parliament (covered under Article 88):** (`[16:18]`)
    *   Can **take part in the proceedings** of the Lok Sabha (and Rajya Sabha).
    *   Can be a **member of any parliamentary committee**.
    *   Can **speak** in the Lok Sabha (and Rajya Sabha).
    *   **Cannot vote** in the Lok Sabha (or Rajya Sabha) because they are not a member.
    *   *UPSC Prelims 2014 Question discussed:* AG can take part in proceedings, be a member of a committee, speak, but not vote. Correct answer: 1, 2, & 3. (`[15:50]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [16:49](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=1009) **1.4 Article 88: Rights of Ministers and Attorney-General as respects Houses**
*   Confirms the Attorney General's right to:
    *   Speak in Parliament.
    *   Take part in proceedings of Parliament.
    *   Be part of any joint sitting.
    *   Be named a member of any committee.
*   Crucially, the AG **shall not be entitled to vote**. (`[17:12]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [17:25](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=1045) **1.5 Article 77: Conduct of Business of the Government of India**
*   All **executive action** of the Government of India shall be expressed to be taken **in the name of the President**. (`[17:39]`)
*   **Article 77 Clause 3:** (`[18:02]`)
    *   The **President shall make rules** for the more convenient transaction of business of the Government of India, and for the allocation among Ministers of the said business.
    *   Constitution does not mention specific ministries (e.g., Home, Defence). (`[18:12]`)
    *   These are established by **Allocation of Business Rules, 1961**, made by the President.

#### 📜 🕒 [18:52](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=1132) **1.6 Article 78: Duties of Prime Minister as respects the furnishing of information to the President, etc.**
*   It shall be the **duty of the Prime Minister**:
    1.  To **communicate to the President all decisions** of the Council of Ministers relating to the administration of the affairs of the Union and proposals for legislation. (`[19:20]`)
        *   *Example:* PM meets President after foreign visits or when important legislation is passed.
    2.  To **furnish such information** relating to the administration of the affairs of the Union and proposals for legislation as the President may call for. (`[19:55]`)
    3.  If the President so requires, to **submit for the consideration of the Council of Ministers any matter on which a decision has been taken by a Minister but which has not been considered by the Council**. (`[20:07]`)
        *   President can ask PM to discuss an individual minister's decision in the full CoM.
*   *UPSC Prelims Question (example):* (`[20:42]`)
    *   Statement 1: Council of Ministers in the center shall be collectively responsible to Parliament. (Incorrect - responsible to Lok Sabha)
    *   Statement 2: Union Ministers shall hold office during the pleasure of the President of India. (Correct - Art 75)
    *   Statement 3: The Prime Minister shall communicate to the President about the proposals for legislation. (Correct - Art 78, verbatim)

### 🏛️ 🕒 [21:45](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=1305) **2.0 Parliament**

#### 📜 🕒 [21:45](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=1305) **2.1 Article 79: Constitution of Parliament**
*   Parliament for the Union shall consist of:
    *   The **President**.
    *   The **Council of States (Rajya Sabha)**.
    *   The **House of the People (Lok Sabha)**. (`[21:58]`)
*   The President is an integral part of both the Executive and the Legislature. (`[22:12]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [22:47](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=1367) **2.2 Article 80: Composition of the Council of States (Rajya Sabha)**
*   **Rajya Sabha consists of:** (`[22:59]`)
    1.  **12 members nominated by the President** from persons having special knowledge or practical experience in:
        *   Literature
        *   Science
        *   Art
        *   Social service
    2.  **Not more than 238 representatives of the States and of the Union Territories**. (`[23:25]`)
*   **Maximum Strength:** 250 (12 nominated + 238 elected). (`[23:47]`)
*   Allocation of seats to states in Rajya Sabha (e.g., UP-31, Telangana-7) is specified in the **Fourth Schedule**. (`[24:07]`)
*   **Election of Rajya Sabha MPs:**
    *   **From States:** (`[24:19]`)
        *   Elected by the **elected members of the Legislative Assembly** of that State.
        *   In accordance with the system of **proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote**.
    *   **From Union Territories (with legislative assemblies like Delhi, Puducherry, J&K):** (`[25:21]`)
        *   Chosen in such manner as **Parliament may by law prescribe**. (`[25:45]`)
        *   Parliament *may* prescribe proportional representation, but it's not mandated by the Constitution for UTs (unlike for states).

#### 📜 🕒 [26:20](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=1580) **2.3 Article 81: Composition of the House of the People (Lok Sabha)**
*   "Subject to the provisions of **Article 331**..." (`[26:32]`)
    *   **Article 331 (now largely defunct):** Allowed President to nominate a maximum of **two members from the Anglo-Indian community** to Lok Sabha if not adequately represented. (`[26:52]`)
    *   📜 **104 th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2019:** Did away with Anglo-Indian reservation in Lok Sabha. (`[27:42]`)
*   **Lok Sabha Composition (Post-104 th Amendment):** (`[28:02]`)
    *   Not more than **530 members chosen by direct election from territorial constituencies in the States**.
    *   Not more than **20 members to represent the Union Territories**.
    *   Current maximum strength: **550** (previously 552 with Anglo-Indian nominees). (`[28:20]`)
*   **Principle of Representation:** (`[28:38]`)
    *   **Parity across states:** Ratio between the number of seats allotted to each State and its population should be, as far as practicable, the same throughout the country (aiming for "one person, one vote").
    *   **Parity within a state:** Each State shall be divided into territorial constituencies such that the ratio between the population of each constituency and the number of seats allotted to it is, as far as practicable, the same throughout the State. (`[29:45]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [30:34](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=1834) **2.4 Article 82: Readjustment after each census (Delimitation)**
*   Upon completion of **each census**, the allocation of seats in Lok Sabha to States and the division of each State into territorial constituencies **shall be readjusted**. (`[30:46]`)
*   This **delimitation exercise** is to be carried out by **such authority and in such manner as Parliament may by law determine**. (`[31:43]`)
    *   This means it's not necessarily the Delimitation Commission; Parliament can assign it to another body (e.g., Election Commission, as done for Assam recently). (`[32:06]`)
*   **Freeze on Delimitation:**
    *   Due to protests from Southern states (who controlled population better) against potential loss of seats to Northern states.
    *   📜 **42 nd Amendment Act (Indira Gandhi):** Froze delimitation (seat allocation based on population) till 2001, using 1971 census data for seat numbers. (`[34:02]`)
    *   📜 **Later Amendment:** Extended this freeze till **after the first census post-2026** (i.e., likely 2031 census for seat numbers). The number of seats will not increase until then. (`[34:13]`)
*   **Internal Delimitation (e.g., 2002 exercise):** (`[34:39]`)
    *   Even if total seats are frozen, internal delimitation can occur for two reasons:
        1.  **Readjustment of SC/ST reserved seats:** Based on updated population figures from censuses, the number and location of seats reserved for SCs and STs are readjusted *within the existing total number of seats*. (`[34:54]`)
        2.  **Redrawing constituency boundaries within states:** To maintain the principle of "one person, one vote" by equalizing the population of constituencies based on a more recent census (e.g., 2001 census was used for this, while 1971 census for total seat allocation). (`[36:20]`)
*   **Current Basis for Seat Allocation & Constituency Division:** (`[39:03]`)
    *   Total number of seats allocated to a state (e.g., UP - 80 seats) is based on **1971 census population figures**.
    *   Division of these seats into constituencies within the state (to ensure population parity per constituency) is based on **2001 census population figures**.
*   **Effect of Readjustment:** (`[38:10]`)
    *   Any readjustment (delimitation) **shall not affect representation in the existing House of the People until its dissolution**. The new seat numbers/boundaries apply to the *next* Lok Sabha.

#### 📜 🕒 [40:38](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=2438) **2.5 Article 83: Duration of Houses of Parliament**
*   **Rajya Sabha (Council of States):** (`[40:38]`)
    *   **Shall not be subject to dissolution** (a permanent House).
    *   As nearly as possible, **one-third of its members shall retire every second year**.
    *   *Note:* Term of a Rajya Sabha MP is 6 years, but this is specified by law (Representation of the People Act), not directly in Art 83. (`[52:26]`)
*   **Lok Sabha (House of the People):** (`[41:16]`)
    *   Normal term is **five years** from the date appointed for its first meeting, unless sooner dissolved.
    *   **During a National Emergency (Proclamation of Emergency is in operation):** (`[41:39]`)
        *   The term of Lok Sabha can be **extended by Parliament by law for a period not exceeding one year at a time**.
        *   This can be done multiple times (e.g., 5 to 6, 6 to 7 years).
        *   If the Emergency is revoked, the extended Lok Sabha **cannot continue beyond a period of six months** after the Proclamation has ceased to operate. (`[42:05]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [42:41](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=2561) **2.6 Article 84: Qualification for membership of Parliament**
*   **Qualifications prescribed by the Constitution:** (`[42:54]`)
    1.  Must be a **citizen of India**.
    2.  Must **make and subscribe before some person authorized by the Election Commission an oath or affirmation** according to the form set out for the purpose in the **Third Schedule**. (`[43:24]`)
        *   Done before the Returning Officer when contesting elections.
    3.  **Age:**
        *   For Rajya Sabha: **Not less than 30 years**. (`[44:32]`)
        *   For Lok Sabha: **Not less than 25 years**.
*   **Other qualifications:** (`[44:43]`)
    *   Parliament may prescribe **additional qualifications by law**.
    *   *Example (from Representation of the People Act, not Constitution):* Must be a registered voter in any parliamentary constituency. (`[44:57]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [45:25](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=2725) **2.7 Article 85: Sessions of Parliament, prorogation and dissolution**
*   **Summoning of Parliament:** (`[45:45]`)
    *   The **President shall from time to time summon each House of Parliament to meet at such time and place as he thinks fit**.
    *   *Crucial detail:* Parliament can meet *anywhere* in India, not necessarily in Delhi in the Parliament building. (`[45:58]`)
    *   **Requirement:** Six months shall not intervene between its last sitting in one session and the date appointed for its first sitting in the next session. (Parliament must meet at least twice a year). (`[46:40]`)
    *   Constitution does *not* mention Budget, Monsoon, Winter sessions; these are based on Rules of Procedure/conventions. (`[46:53]`)
*   **Prorogation:** (`[47:31]`)
    *   The **President may from time to time prorogue the Houses** or either House (terminate a session).
*   **Dissolution:** (`[47:51]`)
    *   The **President may dissolve the House of the People (Lok Sabha)**.
    *   Rajya Sabha is not subject to dissolution.

#### 📜 🕒 [48:03](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=2883) **2.8 Article 86: Right of President to address and send messages to Houses**
*   The President may **address either House of Parliament or both Houses assembled together**, and for that purpose require the attendance of members. (`[48:17]`)
*   The President may **send messages to either House of Parliament**, whether with respect to a Bill then pending in Parliament or otherwise. (`[48:58]`)
*   A House to which any message is so sent **shall with all convenient dispatch consider any matter required by the message to be taken into consideration**.

#### 📜 🕒 [49:33](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=2973) **2.9 Article 87: Special address by the President**
*   Distinct from Article 86 address.
*   **Two occasions for Special Address:** (`[49:46]`)
    1.  At the **commencement of the first session after each general election** to the House of the People.
    2.  At the **commencement of the first session of each year**.
*   On these occasions, the President **shall address both Houses of Parliament assembled together** and inform Parliament of the causes of its summons. (`[50:58]`)
*   The text of this speech is prepared by the **Cabinet**. (`[51:13]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [51:31](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=3091) **2.10 Article 90: Vacation and resignation of, and removal from, the office of Deputy Chairman (Rajya Sabha)**
*   A member holding office as **Deputy Chairman of the Council of States**:
    *   The Deputy Chairman **must be a member** of the Rajya Sabha (unlike the Chairman, who is the Vice-President). (`[51:47]`)
    *   **Shall vacate his office if he ceases to be a member** of the Council. (`[52:12]`)
    *   May at any time **resign** his office by writing to the **Chairman (Vice-President)**. (`[52:50]`)
    *   May be **removed** from his office by a resolution of the Council passed by a **majority of all the then members of the Council (effective majority)**. (`[53:01]`)
        *   Grounds for removal are **not mentioned** in the Constitution.
        *   **14 days' notice** must be given of the intention to move the resolution for removal.

#### 📜 🕒 [53:48](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=3228) **2.11 Article 91: Power of the Deputy Chairman or other person to perform the duties of the office of, or to act as, Chairman (Rajya Sabha)**
*   **Scenario 1: Chairman (Vice-President) is ABSENT:** (`[54:06]`)
    1.  **Deputy Chairman** presides.
    2.  If Deputy Chairman is also absent, such person as determined by the **Rules of Procedure of the Council (Rajya Sabha)** presides. (`[54:39]`)
        *   Rules usually specify a **panel of Vice-Chairpersons** (e.g., 6 members).
        3.  If no one from the panel is present, any other member as determined by the **Council itself** at that time presides. (`[55:38]`)
*   **Scenario 2: Office of Chairman (Vice-President) is VACANT (due to death, resignation, removal):** (`[57:04]`)
    1.  **Deputy Chairman** performs the duties.
    2.  If the office of Deputy Chairman is also vacant (or Deputy Chairman is absent), such member of the Council as the **President may appoint** for the purpose shall perform the duties. (`[57:32]`)
        *   *Key difference:* President appoints, not rules or panel, when Chairman's office is vacant and Deputy is unavailable.

#### 📜 🕒 [58:02](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=3482) **2.12 Article 93: The Speaker and Deputy Speaker of the House of the People (Lok Sabha)**
*   The House of the People **shall, as soon as may be, choose two members of the House to be respectively Speaker and Deputy Speaker**. (`[01:00:00]`)
    *   This makes it **mandatory** to have both a Speaker and a Deputy Speaker.
    *   The date for the election of the Speaker is set by the **President**. (`[01:00:52]`)
    *   The date for the election of the Deputy Speaker is set by the **Speaker**.
*   If the office of Speaker or Deputy Speaker becomes vacant, the House shall choose another member.
*   **Convention vs. Constitution:** (`[01:01:43]`)
    *   Constitution does **not** state that the Speaker must be from the ruling party and Deputy Speaker from the opposition.
    *   Any **member** of Lok Sabha can be chosen for these roles.

#### 📜 🕒 [01:02:24](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=3744) **2.13 Article 94: Vacation and resignation of, and removal from, the offices of Speaker and Deputy Speaker (Lok Sabha)**
*   A member holding office as Speaker
Or Deputy Speaker of the House of the People:
    *   **Shall vacate his office if he ceases to be a member** of the House of the People. (`[01:02:39]`)
    *   May at any time **resign**:
        *   Speaker resigns by writing to the **Deputy Speaker**. (`[01:03:11]`)
        *   Deputy Speaker resigns by writing to the **Speaker**.
    *   May be **removed** from his office by a resolution of the House of the People passed by a **majority of all the then members of the House (effective majority)**. (`[01:03:40]`)
        *   **14 days' notice** must be given of the intention to move the resolution.
*   **Continuity of Speaker's Office upon Dissolution:** (`[01:05:02]`)
    *   **Constitutional Provision (not a convention):** Whenever the House of the People is dissolved, the **Speaker shall not vacate his office until immediately before the first meeting of the House of the People after the dissolution**. (`[01:05:12]`)
    *   Ensures continuity in the office of the Speaker. Mr. Om Birla would resign only before the first meeting of the new Lok Sabha. (`[01:05:26]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [01:05:55](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=3955) **2.14 Article 95: Power of the Deputy Speaker or other person to perform the duties of the office of, or to act as, Speaker (Lok Sabha)**
*   (Similar logic to Article 91 for Rajya Sabha, detailed at `[58:02]`)
*   **Scenario 1: Speaker is ABSENT:** (`[58:15]`)
    1.  **Deputy Speaker** presides.
    2.  If Deputy Speaker is also absent (or office is vacant, e.g., 17 th Lok Sabha had no Deputy Speaker), such person as determined by the **Rules of Procedure of the House (Lok Sabha)** presides. (`[58:27]`)
        *   Rules usually specify a **panel of Chairpersons** (e.g., 10 members).
        3.  If no one from the panel is present, any other member as determined by the **House itself** at that time presides. (`[58:41]`)
*   **Scenario 2: Office of Speaker is VACANT:** (`[58:55]`)
    1.  **Deputy Speaker** performs the duties.
    2.  If the office of Deputy Speaker is also vacant, such member of the House as the **President may appoint** for the purpose shall perform the duties. (`[59:08]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [01:06:07](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=3967) **2.15 Article 100: Voting in Houses, power of Houses to act notwithstanding vacancies and quorum**
*   **Voting on Questions:** (`[01:06:07]`)
    *   All questions at any sitting of either House or joint sitting shall be determined by a **majority of votes of the members present and voting (simple majority)**.
    *   The Speaker or Chairman (or person acting as such) **does not vote in the first instance**. They are excluded when calculating simple majority for a question. (`[01:06:56]`)
    *   They **shall exercise a casting vote** only in the case of an **equality of votes (a tie)**. (`[01:07:37]`)
*   **Quorum:** (`[01:08:07]`)
    *   The minimum number of members required to be present for the House to conduct business.
    *   Until Parliament by law otherwise provides, the quorum to constitute a meeting of either House of Parliament shall be **one-tenth (1/10 th) of the total number of members of the House**. (`[01:08:33]`)
        *   E.g., for Lok Sabha (strength 543), quorum is approx. 55 members.
    *   If there is no quorum, it is the duty of the Speaker/Chairman to **adjourn the House or suspend the meeting** until there is a quorum.
    *   Parliament **can change this quorum requirement by law** (e.g., make it 1/2 or 1/4 th). (`[01:08:59]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [01:09:30](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=4170) **2.16 Article 101: Vacation of seats**
*   **Simultaneous Membership Prohibited:**
    1.  No person shall be a member of **both Houses of Parliament**. If chosen for both, one seat becomes vacant as per law made by Parliament. (`[01:09:45]`)
    2.  No person shall be a member **both of Parliament and of a House of the Legislature of a State**. (`[01:10:12]`)
        *   If chosen for both, their seat in Parliament shall become vacant unless they resign their seat in the State Legislature within a specified period. (`[01:10:27]`)
*   **Absence from Sittings:** (`[01:11:00]`)
    *   If for a period of **sixty days** a member of either House of Parliament is, without permission of the House, absent from all meetings thereof, the **House may declare his seat vacant**.
    *   In
computing the said period of sixty days, **no account shall be taken of any period during which the House is prorogued or is adjourned for more than four consecutive days**. (`[01:11:37]`)
    *   This refers to 60 *Parliamentary calendar days*, not just calendar days.
    *   The House *may* declare the seat vacant (it's not automatic). Often, members (like Amritpal Singh or Engineer Rashid in jail) write to the Committee on Attendance, and their absence is condoned. (`[01:12:05]`)
*   **Resignation:** (`[01:12:45]`)
    *   A member may resign his seat by writing to the **Speaker (for Lok Sabha) or Chairman (for Rajya Sabha)**.
    *   The Speaker/Chairman, if satisfied after inquiry that the resignation is **voluntary and genuine**, shall accept such resignation.
    *   If they believe it is **not voluntary or genuine (coerced)**, they **may not accept** the resignation. (`[01:13:16]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [01:13:46](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=4426) **2.17 Article 105: Powers, privileges, etc., of the Houses of Parliament and of the members and committees thereof**
*   **Freedom of Speech in Parliament:** (`[01:13:46]`)
    *   There shall be **freedom of speech in Parliament**.
    *   This freedom is **absolute** inside Parliament, unlike Article 19 (1)(a) for citizens which has reasonable restrictions. (`[01:14:42]`)
*   **Immunity from Court Proceedings:** (`[01:13:59]`)
    *   No member of Parliament shall be liable to any proceedings in any court in respect of **anything said or any vote given by him in Parliament** or any committee thereof.
    *   No person shall be so liable in respect of the publication by or under the authority of either House of Parliament of any report, paper, votes, or proceedings.
    *   *JMM Bribery Case (P.V. Narasimha Rao vs CBI):* SC initially held that MPs taking bribes to vote were protected. (`[01:14:14]`)
    *   *Sita Soren Case (Recent):* SC overruled the JMM bribery case, stating **bribery is not part of parliamentary privilege**. (`[01:14:25]`)
*   **Internal Regulation by House:**
    *   While immune from courts, members are subject to the **Rules of Procedure of the House**. (`[01:15:38]`)
    *   The House can take action (e.g., expunge remarks, suspend, expel, privilege motions) for unparliamentary conduct. (`[01:15:58]`)
*   **Codification of Privileges:**
    *   Originally, Constitution stated that until defined by Parliament by law, privileges would be those of the **British House of Commons** at the commencement of the Constitution. (`[01:17:08]`)
    *   No specific law codifying all privileges has been enacted by Parliament.
    *   📜 **[[44th amendment\|44th amendment]] Act, 1978:** Amended Article 105 to state that privileges shall be those that were in force *immediately before the coming into force of this amendment* (i.e., effectively the same British privileges continued, but the explicit reference to the British Parliament was dropped for sovereignty reasons). (`[01:18:39]`)
    *   *Example of a privilege (from British practice):* Immunity from arrest in civil cases during the session of Parliament and 40 days before and 40 days after. This does not apply to criminal cases or preventive detention. (`[01:17:21]`) Arrest in criminal cases or contempt of court (non-civil) is possible, but the Speaker/Chairman must be informed.

#### 📜 🕒 [01:19:47](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=4787) **2.18 Article 107: Provisions as to introduction and passing of Bills**
*   **Origin of Bills:**
    *   Subject to provisions of Article 109 (Money Bills) and Article 117 (Financial Bills), a **Bill may originate in either House of Parliament**. (`[01:20:02]`)
    *   Money Bills and Financial Bills (Type I) can only be introduced in **Lok Sabha**.
*   **Passing of Bills:**
    *   A Bill shall not be deemed to have been passed by the Houses of Parliament unless it has been **agreed to by both Houses**, either without amendment or with such amendments only as are agreed to by both Houses. (`[01:20:36]`)
*   **Lapsing of Bills:**
    *   **Prorogation:** A Bill pending in Parliament **shall not lapse** by reason of the prorogation of the Houses. (`[01:21:02]`)
    *   **Dissolution of Lok Sabha:**
        *   A Bill pending in the **Rajya Sabha which has not been passed by the Lok Sabha shall not lapse**. (Origin Rajya Sabha, pending Rajya Sabha). (`[01:21:23]`)
        *   A Bill which is **pending in the Lok Sabha**, OR which having been **passed by the Lok Sabha is pending in the Rajya Sabha**, **shall lapse** on dissolution of Lok Sabha. (Origin Lok Sabha, pending anywhere). (`[01:22:04]`)
    *   **Bills that DO NOT LAPSE on dissolution of Lok Sabha:** (`[01:23:28]`)
        1.  Bill originating in Rajya Sabha and pending in Rajya Sabha (not yet passed by Lok Sabha).
        2.  Bill passed by both Houses and awaiting President's assent.
        3.  Bill on which the President has notified an intention to summon a **joint sitting**.
*   *UPSC Prelims 2024 Question discussed:* (`[01:22:53]`)
    *   Statement 1: Bill pending in Lok Sabha lapses on dissolution. (Correct)
    *   Statement 2: Bill passed by Lok Sabha and pending in Rajya Sabha lapses on dissolution. (Correct)
    *   Statement 3: Bill for which President has notified joint sitting lapses on dissolution. (Incorrect - it survives)
*   A bill on which there is a deadlock *will lapse* if Lok Sabha dissolves, *unless* the President has already notified a joint sitting. The mere existence of a deadlock doesn't save it. (`[01:24:52]`)
*   If President notifies joint sitting, and *then* Lok Sabha dissolves, the bill *still survives* and joint sitting can happen. (`[01:25:46]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [01:26:27](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=5187) **2.19 Article 108: Joint sitting of both Houses in certain cases**
*   **When Joint Sitting is Convened:** (`[01:26:27]`)
    *   If after a Bill has been passed by one House and transmitted to the other House:
        1.  The Bill is **rejected** by the other House; or
        2.  The Houses have **finally disagreed** as to the amendments to be made in the Bill; or
        3.  **More than six months elapse** from the date of the reception of the Bill by the other House without the Bill being passed by it.
    *   The **President may notify** his intention to summon them to meet in a joint sitting to resolve the deadlock.
    *   Notification is on the advice of PM & CoM. (`[01:28:27]`)
*   **Not Applicable to Money Bills:** Joint sitting cannot be called for a Money Bill. (`[01:28:38]`)
*   **Numerical Dominance of Lok Sabha:** In a joint sitting, Lok Sabha usually prevails due to its larger numbers. (`[01:29:06]`)
*   **Instances of Joint Sittings (Historically):** (`[01:29:33]`)
    1.  Dowry Prohibition Bill, 1960.
    2.  Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill, 1977.
    3.  Prevention of Terrorism Bill (POTA), 2002.
*   **Presiding Officer of Joint Sitting (from Rules, not directly Art 108):** (`[01:40:02]`)
    1.  Speaker of Lok Sabha.
    2.  If Speaker absent: Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha. (`[01:40:53]`)
    3.  If Deputy Speaker absent: Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha.
    4.  If Deputy Chairman also absent: Such other person as may be determined by the members present at the sitting. (`[01:41:17]`)
    *   **Chairman of Rajya Sabha (Vice-President) never presides** over a joint sitting as they are not a member of either House. (`[01:41:28]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [01:29:46](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=5386) **2.20 Article 109: Special procedure in respect of Money Bills**
*   **Introduction:**
    *   A Money Bill **shall not be introduced in the Rajya Sabha**. (`[01:30:00]`)
    *   Can only be introduced in **Lok Sabha**.
    *   Requires **prior recommendation of the President** for introduction. (`[01:30:18]`)
    *   Can theoretically be a private member's bill if President gives prior approval (though rare). (`[01:30:31]`)
*   **Role of Rajya Sabha:**
    *   After being passed by Lok Sabha, it is transmitted to Rajya Sabha.
    *   Rajya Sabha has **14 days** to return the Bill to Lok Sabha with its recommendations. (`[01:33:32]`)
    *   Rajya Sabha **cannot reject or amend** a Money Bill. It can only make recommendations. (`[01:32:17]`)
    *   Lok Sabha may **accept or reject all or any of the recommendations** of Rajya Sabha. (`[01:33:43]`)
        *   If Lok Sabha accepts recommendations, Bill is passed as amended.
        *   If Lok Sabha rejects recommendations, Bill is passed in its original form (as passed by Lok Sabha).
    *   If Rajya Sabha does **not return the Bill within 14 days**, it is deemed to have been passed by both Houses in the form it was passed by Lok Sabha. (`[01:32:59]`)
*   **Speaker's Certification:**
    *   If any question arises whether a Bill is a Money Bill or not, the **decision of the Speaker of the Lok Sabha shall be final**. (This certificate is attached when Bill goes to Rajya Sabha). (`[01:31:53]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [01:34:13](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=5653) **2.21 Article 111: Assent to Bills**
*   When a Bill has been passed by the Houses of Parliament, it is presented to the **President**. (`[01:34:30]`)
*   The President shall declare either:
    1.  That he **assents** to the Bill (Bill becomes Act).
    2.  That he **withholds assent** therefrom. (`[01:35:11]`)
*   **Returning a Bill (Suspensive Veto mechanism):**
    *   If the President withholds assent from a Bill (other than a Money Bill), he **shall return the Bill as soon as possible** to the Houses with a message requesting reconsideration. (`[01:35:50]`)
    *   A **Money Bill cannot be returned** by the President for reconsideration. (`[01:36:13]`) (President can assent or withhold assent to a Money Bill).
    *   If the Bill is passed again by the Houses with or without amendment and presented to the President for assent, the **President shall not withhold assent therefrom** (i.e., must give assent). (`[01:37:15]`)
*   **Veto Powers (Constitutional Interpretation, not explicit terms):** (`[01:37:25]`)
    *   The terms "absolute veto," "suspensive veto," "pocket veto" are **not explicitly mentioned** in the Constitution.
    *   Constitutional experts interpret these powers from Article 111.
    *   No time limit is specified for the President to act (give assent, withhold, or return). This 'no time limit' is the basis for "pocket veto" interpretation.

#### 📜 🕒 [01:39:01](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=5941) **2.22 Article 118: Rules of procedure**
*   Each House of Parliament **may make rules for regulating its procedure and the conduct of its business**. (`[01:39:01]`)
*   **Joint Sitting Rules:** (`[01:39:25]`)
    *   The **President, after consultation with the Chairman of Rajya Sabha and the Speaker of Lok Sabha, may make rules** as to the procedure with respect to joint sittings.

#### 📜 🕒 [01:41:38](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=6098) **2.23 Article 121: Restriction on discussion in Parliament**
*   **No discussion shall take place in Parliament with respect to the conduct of any Judge of the Supreme Court or of a High Court** in the discharge of his duties. (`[01:41:38]`)
*   **Exception:** Upon a motion for presenting an address to the President praying for the **removal of the Judge**. (`[01:41:54]`) (i.e., during impeachment proceedings).

#### 📜 🕒 [01:42:34](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=6154) **2.24 Article 122: Courts not to inquire into proceedings of Parliament**
*   The **validity of any proceedings in Parliament shall not be called in question on the ground of any alleged irregularity of procedure**. (`[01:42:34]`)
*   No officer or member of Parliament vested with powers for regulating procedure or conduct of business shall be subject to the jurisdiction of any court for the exercise of those powers.
*   Courts can review a law for violation of fundamental rights or legislative competence, but not the internal procedures of Parliament (e.g., why voice vote, no debate). (`[01:43:02]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [01:43:50](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=6230) **2.25 Article 123: Power of President to promulgate Ordinances during recess of Parliament**
*   If at any time, **except when both Houses of Parliament are in session**, the President is satisfied that circumstances exist which render it necessary for him to take immediate action, he may promulgate such **Ordinances** as the circumstances appear to him to require. (`[01:44:04]`)
    *   Can be promulgated if even *one* House is not in session.
*   An Ordinance shall have the **same force and effect as an Act of Parliament**.
*   **Limitations:**
    *   President can promulgate ordinances on subjects where **Parliament has power to make laws** (Union List, Concurrent List). (`[01:44:51]`)
    *   An Ordinance **cannot be used to amend the Constitution**. (`[01:44:37]`)
    *   An Ordinance (like any law under Art 13) cannot take away or abridge fundamental rights. (`[01:45:17]`)
*   **Laying before Parliament and Duration:**
    *   Every such Ordinance must be laid before both Houses of Parliament.
    *   It shall cease to operate at the expiration of **six weeks from the reassembly of Parliament**, or if, before the expiration of that period, resolutions disapproving it are passed by both Houses.
    *   It can also be withdrawn at any time by the President.
*   (Similar power for Governor under Article 213).

### ⚖️ 🕒 [01:45:49](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=6349) **3.0 The Union Judiciary (The Supreme Court)**

#### 📜 🕒 [01:45:49](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=6349) **3.1 Article 124: Establishment and constitution of Supreme Court**
*   There shall be a **Supreme Court of India** consisting of a **Chief Justice of India (CJI)**. (`[01:45:49]`)
*   **Number of Judges:**
    *   Originally: CJI and **not more than seven other Judges**. (`[01:46:03]`)
    *   **Parliament may by law prescribe a larger number** of Judges. (`[01:46:21]`)
    *   This law to increase the number of judges is an **ordinary law** (passed by simple majority) and is **not deemed an amendment to the Constitution** under Article 368. (`[01:47:39]`)
    *   Current strength (as discussed in video): 33 + CJI.
*   *UPSC Prelims 2014 Question discussed:* Power to increase number of SC judges is vested in Parliament. (`[01:47:58]`)
*   **Appointment of Judges:**
    *   (Video mentions collegium system is followed due to court judgments, not explicitly from Constitution text in Art 124 as it stands modified by NJAC related amendments which were struck down. The text of Art 124 (2) would refer to consultation with judges).
    *   **NJAC (National Judicial Appointments Commission):** (`[01:50:57]`)
        *   Introduced by **99 th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2014**.
        *   Aimed to replace the collegium system.
        *   Declared **unconstitutional** by the Supreme Court in the **Fourth Judges Case (SCORA vs Union of India, 2015)** by a 4:1 majority. (`[01:52:00]`)
        *   Even if declared unconstitutional, the text (e.g., references to NJAC) remains in the Constitution document but is unenforceable. (`[01:52:24]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [01:48:30](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=6510) **3.2 Article 126: Appointment of acting Chief Justice**
*   When the office of CJI is **vacant**, or when the CJI is, by reason of **absence or otherwise, unable to perform the duties** of his office. (`[01:48:30]`)
*   The duties shall be performed by such one of the other Judges of the Court as the **President may appoint** for the purpose. (`[01:48:56]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [01:49:09](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=6549) **3.3 Article 127: Appointment of ad hoc Judges**
*   If at any time there should not be a **quorum of the Judges of the Supreme Court** available to hold or continue any session of the Court. (`[01:49:09]`)
*   The **CJI may, with the previous consent of the President** and after consultation with the Chief Justice of the High Court concerned, request in writing the attendance at the sittings of the Court, as an **ad hoc Judge**, for such period as may be necessary, of a **Judge of a High Court duly qualified for appointment as a Judge of the Supreme Court**. (`[01:49:54]`)
    *   Used if a larger bench is needed (e.g., to review Kesavananda Bharati) and there aren't enough SC judges. (`[01:49:27]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [01:50:23](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=6623) **3.4 Article 128: Attendance of retired Judges at sittings of the Supreme Court**
*   The **CJI may at any time, with the previous consent of the President**, request any person who has held the office of a Judge of the Supreme Court or of the Federal Court or of a High Court (and is qualified for SC) to sit and act as a Judge of the Supreme Court. (`[01:52:45]`)
*   The retired judge so requested **must consent** to sit and act. (`[01:53:10]`)
*   (The video text shows "NJAC may at any time..." this is due to the unamended text from a Bare Act that includes the 99 th Amendment. Pre-99 th Amendment, it would be CJI).

#### 📜 🕒 [01:57:23](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=7043) **3.5 Article 129: Supreme Court to be a court of record**
*   The Supreme Court shall be a **court of record** and shall have all the powers of such a court including the **power to punish for contempt of itself**. (`[01:57:23]`)
*   **Implications of Court of Record:**
    1.  Its judgments, proceedings, and acts are enrolled for perpetual memory and testimony.
    2.  These records are admitted to be of evidentiary value and cannot be questioned when produced before any court.
    3.  It has power to punish for contempt.

#### 📜 🕒 [01:58:06](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=7086) **3.6 Article 130: Seat of Supreme Court**
*   The Supreme Court shall sit in **Delhi or in such other place or places, as the Chief Justice of India may, with the approval of the President, from time to time, appoint**. (`[01:58:06]`)
    *   SC can sit in Delhi and other cities simultaneously if CJI decides with President's approval.

#### 📜 🕒 [01:58:48](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=7128) **3.7 Article 131: Original jurisdiction of the Supreme Court**
*   The Supreme Court shall, to the exclusion of any other court, have **original jurisdiction** in any dispute: (`[01:58:48]`)
    *   (a) Between the **Government of India and one or more States**; or
    *   (b) Between the **Government of India and any State or States on one side and one or more other States on the other**; or
    *   (c) Between **two or more States**.
*   This jurisdiction is **original and exclusive**. (`[01:59:08]`)
    *   **Original:** Cases can be filed directly in the SC.
    *   **Exclusive:** Only SC can hear these specific federal disputes.
*   *Distinction with Fundamental Rights jurisdiction (Art 32):*
    *   Art 32 is original but *not exclusive* (High Courts also have writ jurisdiction under Art 226). (`[01:59:53]`)
*   *UPSC Prelims Question discussed:* SC's power to decide disputes between Centre and States falls under its Original Jurisdiction. (`[02:01:09]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [02:01:23](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=7283) **3.8 Article 138: Enlargement of the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court**
*   The Supreme Court shall have such further jurisdiction and powers with respect to any of the matters in the Union List as **Parliament may by law confer**. (`[02:01:23]`)
*   It shall have such further jurisdiction and powers with respect to any matter as the Government of India and the Government of any State may by special agreement confer, if **Parliament by law** provides for the exercise of such jurisdiction.
*   Essentially, **Parliament by law can enlarge** the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court.

#### 📜 🕒 [02:01:53](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=7313) **3.9 Article 139: Conferment on the Supreme Court of powers to issue certain writs**
*   **Parliament may by law confer on the Supreme Court power to issue directions, orders or writs, including writs in the nature of habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, quo warranto and certiorari, or any of them, for any purposes other than those mentioned in clause (2) of Article 32**. (`[02:02:08]`)
    *   Article 32 empowers SC to issue writs for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.
    *   Article 139 allows Parliament to empower SC to issue writs for **other purposes** (e.g., for enforcement of legal rights), which it currently does not have directly (High Courts have this wider writ jurisdiction under Art 226). (`[02:02:37]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [02:03:02](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=7382) **3.10 Article 141: Law declared by Supreme Court to be binding on all courts**
*   The **law declared by the Supreme Court shall be binding on all courts** within the territory of India. (`[02:03:15]`)
    *   This is the basis for the precedential value of SC judgments and how interpretations like the collegium system (from SC judgments) become law.

#### 📜 🕒 [02:03:41](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=7421) **3.11 Article 142: Enforcement of decrees and orders of Supreme Court and orders as to discovery, etc.**
*   The Supreme Court in the exercise of its jurisdiction may pass such **decree or make such order as is necessary for doing complete justice** in any cause or matter pending before it. (`[02:03:41]`)
*   Any decree so passed or order so made shall be enforceable throughout the territory of India.
*   This gives SC vast powers to deviate from existing laws or procedures if needed to ensure "complete justice".
    *   *Example:* Dalit boy denied IIT admission due to late fee payment; SC used Art 142 to ensure admission. (`[02:04:05]`)
    *   *Example:* Manipur Forest Minister (defector) removed by SC using Art 142, despite established procedures involving Governor/Speaker. (`[02:05:28]`)
*   *UPSC Prelims 2019 Question discussed:* Art 142 implies SC is not constrained by ordinary laws made by Parliament in exercising its powers for complete justice. (`[02:04:55]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [02:06:44](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=7604) **3.12 Article 143: Power of President to consult Supreme Court (Advisory Jurisdiction)**
*   If at any time it appears to the **President** that a question of law or fact has arisen, or is likely to arise, which is of such a nature and such public importance that it is expedient to obtain the **opinion of the Supreme Court** upon it, he may refer the question to that Court for consideration. (`[02:06:44]`)
*   **Nature of Advice:**
    *   The advice (opinion) tendered by the SC is **not binding on the President** (theoretically). (`[02:07:21]`)
    *   However, it carries significant weight and is usually followed.
*   **Obligation of SC to give advice:** (`[02:08:39]`)
    *   If the matter referred relates to a **pre-Constitutional treaty, agreement, covenant, engagement, sanad or other similar instrument (Article 131 Proviso matters)**, it is **binding on the SC to give its opinion**.
    *   If it's a **post-Constitutional matter** (general question of law/fact), it is **not binding on the SC to give its opinion** (SC may decline).
    *   *Example of advisory opinion:* Third Judges Case (1998) on appointment of judges, which established the collegium composition. (`[02:07:44]`)
*   This advisory jurisdiction is not present in the US Constitution. (`[02:10:06]`)

### 📊 🕒 [02:10:25](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=7825) **4.0 Comptroller and Auditor-General of India (CAG)**

#### 📜 🕒 [02:10:43](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=7843) **4.1 Article 148: Comptroller and Auditor-General of India**
*   There shall be a **Comptroller and Auditor-General of India**. (Constitutional post) (`[02:10:53]`)
*   **Appointment:**
    *   Appointed by the **President by warrant under his hand and seal**. (`[02:11:07]`)
    *   "Warrant under his hand and seal" means the President cannot delegate this power of appointment. (`[02:11:18]`)
*   **Removal:**
    *   Shall only be removed from office in **like manner and on the like grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court**. (`[02:11:58]`)
        *   Grounds for SC Judge removal: **Proved misbehaviour or incapacity**. (`[02:12:11]`)
        *   Manner: Resolution by both Houses of Parliament by special majority, then removed by President (detailed in Judges Inquiry Act, 1968).
*   **Oath:**
    *   Subscribes to an oath or affirmation (form in **Third Schedule**) before the President, or some person appointed by him. (`[02:13:33]`)
*   **Salary and Conditions of Service:**
    *   Determined by **Parliament by law**. (`[02:14:00]`) (Cf. Attorney General's remuneration by President).
    *   Neither salary, nor rights in respect of leave, pension or age of retirement **shall be varied to his disadvantage after his appointment**. (Ensures independence). (`[02:14:14]`)
*   **Ineligibility for Further Office:** (`[02:14:52]`)
    *   The CAG shall, on ceasing to hold office, be **ineligible for further office either under the Government of India or under the Government of any State**.
    *   This does not bar appointment as Governor or election as President, as these are not considered "employment" under government. (`[02:15:16]`)
*   **Expenses:**
    *   Salaries, allowances, and pension of CAG and administrative expenses of his office are **charged upon the Consolidated Fund of India**. (Discussed but not voted by Parliament). (`[02:15:42]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [02:16:30](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=8190) **4.2 Article 151: Audit reports**
*   Reports of the CAG relating to the accounts of the **Union** shall be submitted to the **President**, who shall cause them to be laid before each House of Parliament. (`[02:16:43]`)
*   Reports of the CAG relating to the accounts of a **State** shall be submitted to the **Governor** of the State, who shall cause them to be laid before the Legislature of the State. (`[02:16:59]`)
*   These reports are then examined by the **Public Accounts Committee (PAC)** of Parliament/State Legislature. (`[02:17:13]`)
    *   Chairman of PAC is conventionally from the Opposition.
    *   CAG is often called the "friend, philosopher, and guide" of the PAC. (`[02:18:12]`)

### 🏛️ 🕒 [02:18:27](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=8307) **5.0 The States (Part VI)**
*   Part VI deals with States. (Part I: Union & Territory, II: Citizenship, III: FRs, IV: DPSPs, IVA: FDs, V: Union).
*   State machinery largely mirrors Union machinery with some exceptions. (`[02:19:07]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [02:19:17](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=8357) **5.1 Article 153: Governors of States**
*   There shall be a **Governor for each State**. (`[02:19:17]`)
*   📜 **7 th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956:** Added a proviso that nothing in this article shall prevent the appointment of the **same person as Governor for two or more States**. (`[02:19:29]`)
    *   This was not in the original Constitution.

#### 📜 🕒 [02:20:13](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=8413) **5.2 Article 156: Term of office of Governor**
*   The Governor shall hold office **during the pleasure of the President**. (Lacks security of tenure). (`[02:20:13]`)
*   May, by writing under his hand addressed to the President, **resign** his office.
*   Subject to the above, a Governor shall hold office for a **term of five years** from the date on which he enters upon his office. (`[02:20:28]`)
    *   However, the "pleasure of the President" (effectively PM & CoM) means they can be removed or transferred before 5 years. (`[02:20:40]`)
    *   *Shamsher Singh vs State of Punjab:* "Satisfaction of the President" means satisfaction of CoM. (`[02:20:55]`)
*   *UPSC Prelims 2013 Question discussed:* (`[02:22:04]`)
    *   Statement 1: Same person cannot be Governor for 2+ states. (Incorrect - 7 th Am.)
    *   Statement 2: HC judges appointed by Governor. (Incorrect - President)
    *   Statement 3: CM in UT with legislature appointed by LG. (Incorrect - President, though LG might administer oath)
    *   Statement 4: No procedure in Constitution for removal of Governor. (Correct - holds office during pleasure).

#### 📜 🕒 [02:23:27](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=8607) **5.3 Article 157: Qualifications for appointment as Governor**
*   No person shall be eligible for appointment as Governor unless:
    1.  He is a **citizen of India**.
    2.  He has completed the **age of thirty-five years**. (`[02:23:41]`)
*   **Conventions (not in Constitution):** (`[02:23:56]`)
    *   Governor should be an outsider (not from the state he is appointed to).
    *   Chief Minister of the state should be consulted before appointing the Governor.

#### 📜 🕒 [02:24:17](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=8657) **5.4 Article 158: Conditions of Governor’s office**
*   The Governor **shall not be a member of either House of Parliament or of a House of the Legislature of any State**. (`[02:24:31]`)
    *   If such a member is appointed Governor, his seat in that House shall be deemed to have been **vacated on the date on which he enters upon his office as Governor**. (`[02:24:44]`)
*   The Governor **shall not hold any other office of profit**. (`[02:25:11]`)
*   If the same person is appointed Governor of two or more States, the emoluments and allowances payable to the Governor shall be **allocated among the States in such proportion as the President may by order determine**. (`[02:25:21]`)
*   Emoluments and allowances **shall not be diminished** during his term of office. (`[02:26:02]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [02:26:17](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=8777) **5.5 Article 163: Council of Ministers to aid and advise Governor**
*   There shall be a **Council of Ministers with the Chief Minister at the head to aid and advise the Governor** in the exercise of his functions. (`[02:26:17]`)
*   **Exception for Discretionary Powers:** **except in so far as he is by or under this Constitution required to exercise his functions or any of them in his discretion**. (`[02:26:58]`)
*   **Governor's Decision on Discretion Final:** If any question arises whether any matter is or is not a matter as respects which the Governor is by or under this Constitution required to act in his discretion, the **decision of the Governor in his discretion shall be final**, and the validity of anything done by the Governor shall not be called in question on the ground that he ought or ought not to have acted in his discretion. (`[02:27:21]`)
    *   Discretionary powers are not fully codified, leading to potential clashes (e.g., Punjab, Tamil Nadu examples given). (`[02:27:45]`)
*   Advice tendered by Ministers to the Governor **shall not be inquired into in any court**. (Similar to Art 74 (2)). (`[02:31:48]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [02:32:08](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=9128) **5.6 Article 164: Other provisions as to Ministers (State)**
*   **Appointment of Chief Minister:** The Chief Minister shall be appointed by the **Governor**. (`[02:32:08]`)
    *   Governor has **situational discretion** if no party has a clear majority (hung assembly). (`[02:32:19]`)
    *   *Examples of alleged misuse of discretion in Karnataka (Vajubhai Vala inviting Yeddyurappa) and Goa (Mridula Sinha inviting BJP alliance) discussed.* (`[02:33:22]`)
*   **Appointment of Other Ministers:** Other Ministers shall be appointed by the **Governor on the advice of the Chief Minister**. (`[02:35:31]`)
    *   No discretion for Governor here.
*   **Minister for Tribal Welfare:** In States of **Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh and Odisha**, there **shall be a Minister in charge of tribal welfare** who may in addition be in charge of the welfare of the Scheduled Castes and backward classes or any other work. (`[02:35:54]`)
*   **Strength of Council of Ministers (State):** (`[02:36:21]`)
    *   📜 **91 st Amendment Act, 2003:** Total number of Ministers, including the Chief Minister, in the Council of Ministers in a State **shall not exceed fifteen per cent** of the total number of members of the Legislative Assembly of that State.
    *   **Minimum Strength:** Provided that the number of Ministers, including the Chief Minister, in a State shall **not be less than twelve**. (`[02:36:49]`)
    *   *Exception for Delhi:* Delhi has CM + 6 ministers (total 7, which is 10% of 70 Assembly seats) due to special provisions in Article 239 AA, not this general rule. (`[02:37:20]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [02:37:34](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=9454) **5.7 Article 169: Abolition or creation of Legislative Councils in States**
*   Parliament may by law provide for the **abolition of the Legislative Council** of a State having such a Council or for the **creation of such a Council** in a State having no such Council. (`[02:37:34]`)
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  If the **Legislative Assembly of the State passes a resolution to that effect by a majority of the total membership of the Assembly AND by a majority of not less than two-thirds of the members of the Assembly present and voting (special majority)**. (`[02:38:59]`)
    2.  Parliament can then pass a law (by simple majority). (`[02:40:43]`)
*   Such a law made by Parliament **shall not be deemed to be an amendment of this Constitution** for the purposes of Article 368. (`[02:40:19]`)
*   **States with Legislative Councils (Bicameral):** Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Maharashtra, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Karnataka (6 states). (`[02:38:07]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [02:40:59](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=9659) **5.8 Article 182: The Chairman and Deputy Chairman of the Legislative Council**
*   The Legislative Council of every State having such a Council **shall, as soon as may be, choose two members of the Council to be respectively Chairman and Deputy Chairman** thereof. (`[02:40:59]`)
    *   Both Chairman and Deputy Chairman of Legislative Council are **elected/chosen from among the members** of the Council. (`[02:41:45]`)
    *   This is different from Rajya Sabha where Chairman is ex-officio (Vice President).
    *   *UPSC Prelims Question context:* A statement that Chairman LC is nominated while Dy. Chairman is elected would be incorrect. Both are chosen. (`[02:41:59]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [02:42:11](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=9731) **5.9 Article 197: Restriction on powers of Legislative Council as to Bills other than Money Bills**
*   Legislative Council (LC) is considered a **secondary chamber**, with inferior powers compared to Legislative Assembly (LA), unlike Rajya Sabha which is a second chamber. (`[02:43:14]`)
*   **Scenario 1: Bill originates in LC, passed by LC, but rejected by LA:**
    *   The Bill is **dead**. No joint sitting provisions for State Legislatures. (`[02:42:59]`)
*   **Scenario 2: Bill originates in LA, passed by LA, sent to LC:** (`[02:43:55]`)
    *   LC options: Pass, Reject, Not act, Amend.
    *   If LC **rejects** the Bill, or **amends** it in a way LA doesn't agree, or **does not pass it within three months**:
        *   LA can pass the Bill **again**.
    *   When LA passes the Bill for the **second time** and sends it back to LC:
        *   If LC **rejects** it again, or **does not pass it within one month**, or **amends** it again in a way LA doesn't agree:
            *   The Bill shall be**deemed to have been passed by both Houses in the form in which it was passed by
the Legislative Assembly for the second time**. (`[02:45:31]`, `[02:47:01]`)
    *   Essentially, LC can delay an ordinary bill for a maximum of **3 months + 1 month = 4 months**.
*   This procedure does **not apply to Money Bills**, where LC has even more limited powers (similar to Rajya Sabha - 14 days to make recommendations, cannot reject or amend). (`[02:46:33]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [02:47:30](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=10050) **5.10 Article 200: Assent to Bills (Governor)**
*   When a Bill has been passed by the Legislative Assembly of a State (or by both Houses in case of bicameral legislature), it is presented to the **Governor**. (`[02:47:46]`)
*   The Governor shall declare either:
    1.  That he **assents** to the Bill.
    2.  That he **withholds assent** therefrom. (`[02:48:08]`)
    3.  That he **reserves the Bill for the consideration of the President**. (`[02:48:42]`)
*   **Returning a Bill (Suspensive Veto mechanism for Governor):**
    *   If the Governor withholds assent from a Bill (other than a Money Bill), he **may, as soon as possible, return the Bill** to the House (or Houses) with a message requesting reconsideration.
    *   If the Bill is passed again by the House (s) with or without amendment and presented to the Governor for assent, the **Governor shall not withhold assent therefrom** (must give assent).
*   **Reservation for President's Consideration:** (`[02:48:42]`)
    *   This is a significant power of the Governor.
    *   A Governor **can reserve even a Money Bill** for the consideration of the President. (`[02:49:19]`)
    *   This is generally a **discretionary power** of the Governor. (`[02:49:31]`)
    *   **Mandatory Reservation:** It is **mandatory** for the Governor to reserve any Bill for the President's consideration if, in his opinion, it would, if it became law, **so derogate from the powers of the High Court as to endanger the position which that Court is by this Constitution designed to fill**. (`[02:50:00]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [02:50:31](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=10231) **5.11 Article 201: Bills reserved for consideration (President)**
*   When a Bill is reserved by a Governor for the consideration of the President, the **President shall declare** either:
    1.  That he **assents** to the Bill.
    2.  That he **withholds assent** therefrom. (`[02:51:09]`)
*   **President's Power to Return a Reserved Bill (other than Money Bill):**
    *   If the Bill is not a Money Bill, the President may direct the Governor to **return the Bill** to the House (s) of the State Legislature with a message for reconsideration. (`[02:51:37]`)
    *   The State Legislature **shall reconsider such Bill accordingly within a period of six months** from the date of receipt of such message. (`[02:51:51]`)
    *   If it is again passed by the House (s) with or without amendment, it shall be **presented again to the President for his consideration**. (Constitution is silent on whether President is bound to assent now, unlike Governor's case in Art 200).

### ⚖️ 🕒 [02:52:35](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=10355) **6.0 The High Courts in the States**

#### 📜 🕒 [02:52:35](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=10355) **6.1 Article 214: High Courts for States**
*   There **shall be a High Court for each State**. (`[02:52:35]`)
*   However, **Article 231** (not explicitly detailed but implied by speaker) allows for **common/joint High Courts for two or more States**, or for two or more States and a Union Territory.
    *   This explains why India has 25 High Courts for 28 States and 8 UTs. (`[02:53:03]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [02:53:14](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=10394) **6.2 Article 215: High Courts to be courts of record**
*   Every High Court shall be a **court of record** and shall have all the powers of such a court including the **power to punish for contempt of itself**. (`[02:53:14]`)
*   District courts are **not courts of record**. If contempt of a district court occurs, the High Court of that state can punish on its behalf. (`[02:53:48]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [02:54:21](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=10461) **6.3 Article 216: Constitution of High Courts**
*   Every High Court shall consist of a **Chief Justice and such other Judges as the President may from time to time deem it necessary to appoint**. (`[02:54:21]`)
    *   The power to determine the number of judges (strength) of a High Court lies with the **President** (unlike Supreme Court, where Parliament decides). (`[02:54:54]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [02:55:19](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=10519) **6.4 Article 220: Restriction on practice after being a permanent Judge**
*   No person who has held office as a **permanent Judge of a High Court** shall plead or act in any court or before any authority in India **except the Supreme Court and the other High Courts**. (`[02:55:19]`)
    *   "Other High Courts" means High Courts where they have **not served** as a permanent judge. (`[02:55:59]`)
    *   *Example:* A retired permanent judge of Gujarat HC cannot practice in Gujarat HC, but can practice in Bombay HC (if not served there) and the Supreme Court.

#### 📜 🕒 [01:54:26](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=6866) **6.5 Article 223: Appointment of acting Chief Justice (High Court)**
*   (Mentioned in comparison to SC's Acting CJI at `[01:54:26]`)
*   When the office of Chief Justice of a High Court is **vacant** or when any such Chief Justice is, by reason of **absence or otherwise, unable to perform the duties** of his office.
*   The duties shall be performed by such one of the other Judges of the Court as the **President may appoint** for the purpose.

#### 📜 🕒 [01:54:57](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=6897) **6.6 Article 224: Appointment of additional and acting Judges (High Court)**
*   **Additional Judges:** (`[01:54:57]`)
    *   If by reason of any **temporary increase in the business** of a High Court or by reason of **arrears of work** therein, it appears to the President that the number of the Judges of that Court should be for the time being increased.
    *   The **President may appoint duly qualified persons to be additional Judges** of the Court for such period **not exceeding two years**.
*   **Acting Judges:** (`[01:56:10]`)
    *   When any Judge of a High Court **other than the Chief Justice** is by reason of absence or for any other reason unable to perform the duties of his office or is appointed to act temporarily as Chief Justice.
    *   The **President may appoint a duly qualified person to act as a Judge** of that Court until the permanent Judge has resumed his duties.
*   No person appointed as an additional or acting Judge shall hold office after attaining the age of **sixty-two years**. (`[01:56:56]`)
*   *Summary of types of judges:* (`[01:57:10]`)
    *   **Supreme Court:** Acting CJI, Ad-hoc Judges, Retired Judges attending.
    *   **High Courts:** Acting Chief Justice, Additional Judges, Acting Judges. (No "ad-hoc" judges in HCs as per this terminology).

#### 📜 🕒 [02:56:22](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=10582) **6.7 Article 230 & 231: Extension of jurisdiction of High Courts to Union territories & Establishment of a common High Court for two or more States**
*   **Article 230:** **Parliament may by law extend the jurisdiction of a High Court to, or exclude the jurisdiction of a High Court from, any Union territory**. (`[02:57:35]`)
    *   This power lies with **Parliament**.
    *   Only Delhi and J&K (UTs) have their own High Courts. Others are under jurisdiction of neighboring state HCs (e.g., Daman & Diu under Bombay HC, Puducherry under Madras HC etc.). (`[02:56:57]`)
*   **Article 231:** Parliament may by law establish a common High Court for two or more States or for two or more States and a Union territory.

*   Introduced in 1962 (primarily for Puducherry). (`[03:02:45]`)
*   **Parliament may by law create** for the Union territory of **Puducherry**:
    *   (a) A body, whether elected or partly nominated and partly elected, to function as a **Legislature** for the Union territory, or
    *   (b) A **Council of Ministers**,
    *   or both, with such constitution, powers and functions as may be specified in the law. (`[03:01:20]`)
*   The **Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019**, states that provisions of Article 239 A shall apply to the UT of Jammu and Kashmir (meaning J&K can have a legislature/CoM similar to Puducherry). (`[03:06:40]`)

#### 📜 🕒 [03:04:27](https://www.youtube.com/live/U-nOErZNAeM?feature=shared&t=11067) **8.3 Article 239 AA: Special provisions with respect to Delhi**

*   The Union territory of Delhi shall be called the **National Capital Territory of Delhi (NCTD)**. (`[03:04:43]`)
*   There shall be a **Legislative Assembly** for the NCTD (70 seats). (`[03:04:57]`)
*   There shall be a **Council of Ministers consisting of not more than ten per cent. Of the total number of members in the Legislative Assembly (i.e., 7 ministers including CM)**, with the Chief Minister at the head to aid and advise the Lieutenant Governor. (`[03:05:09]`)
*   **Legislative Powers of Delhi Assembly:** Can make laws on matters in State List and Concurrent List, **EXCEPT for matters relating to Public Order, Police, and Land**. (`[03:05:52]`)
*   Chief Minister shall be appointed by the **President** (LG administers oath). Other ministers appointed by President on advice of CM. (`[03:06:24]`)

*   The Constitution begins with the **Preamble**.
*   **Part I**: The Union and its territory (names and details of states and UTs).
*   **Part II**: Citizenship.
*   **Part III**: Fundamental Rights.
*   **Part IV**: Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP).
*   **Part IVA**: Fundamental Duties.
    *   Originally not part of the Constitution.
    *   Added by the **42 nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976**.

#### 🔢 🕒 [03:42](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=222) **1.2 Structure and Numbering of Parts**
*   There are **25 Parts** in the Constitution, but the numbering goes up to **22**.
*   Logical flow after Fundamental Duties in a federal polity:
    1.  Union
    2.  States
    3.  Local Government (Panchayati Institutions, Municipalities, Cooperative Societies)
    4.  Special provisions for some states (Scheduled Areas, Tribal Areas).

#### ➡️ 🕒 [04:35](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=275) **1.3 Sequence of Parts (Post Fundamental Duties)**
*   **Part V**: The Union (follows Part IVA - Fundamental Duties).
*   **Part VI**: The States.
*   **Part VII**:
    *   Originally part of the Constitution.
    *   Omitted/deleted by the **7 th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956**.
    *   This amendment reorganized states in India.
    *   Prior to this, territories were classified as Part A, Part B, Part C, Part D.
        *   **Part A**: Big provinces (e.g., Bombay, Madras).
        *   **Part B**: Princely states.
        *   **Part C**: Areas under Chief Commissioners during British times.
        *   **Part D**: Andaman and Nicobar Islands.
    *   The **States Reorganisation Act, 1956** abolished this A, B, C, D differentiation.
    *   Created **14 states and 6 Union Territories** in 1956.
    *   Part VII, dealing with States in Part B of the First Schedule, was deleted.
*   **Part VIII**: Union Territories (follows states).
*   **Part IX**: Panchayats.
    *   Added by the **73 rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992** (often cited as 1993 due to enforcement).
*   **Part IXA**: Municipalities.
    *   Added by the **74 th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992** (often cited as 1993 due to enforcement).
*   **Part IXB**: Cooperative Societies.
    *   Added by the **97 th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2011**.
*   **Recap of initial logical flow**:
    1.  Part I: Union and its Territory
    2.  Part II: Citizenship
    3.  Part III: Fundamental Rights
    4.  Part IV: Directive Principles
    5.  Part IVA: Fundamental Duties
    6.  Part V: Union
    7.  Part VI: States
    8.  (Part VII: Omitted)
    9.  Part VIII: Union Territories
    10. Part IX: Panchayats
    11. Part IXA: Municipalities
    12. Part IXB: Cooperative Societies

### 🗓️ 🕒 [11:58](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=718) **2.0 Schedules of the Constitution**
*   Initially **8 schedules** at the commencement of the Constitution.
*   **4 more schedules** were added later.
*   Total today: **12 schedules**.

#### ❓ 🕒 [12:23](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=743) **2.1 What is a Schedule?**
*   A schedule is an **explanatory memorandum of an article**.
*   Added at the end if an article needs more explanation to keep the article itself lean (cosmetic measure).
*   Schedules are part of the Constitution.

#### 📜 🕒 [12:23](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=743) **2.2 Overview of Schedules 1-4**
*   **Schedule 1**:
    *   Names and details of **States and Union Territories**.
    *   Corresponds to **Article 1**.
    *   Includes territorial extent of states and UTs.
*   **Schedule 2**:
    *   **Salaries and emoluments** of the President and other constitutional functionaries.
*   **Schedule 3**:
    *   **Oath or Affirmation**.
        *   **Oath**: For a believer (swears in the name of God).
        *   **Affirmation**: For a non-believer (solemnly affirms).
    *   *Does not include* oath for:
        *   President of India
        *   Vice President of India
        *   Governor of a State
        *   (These are mentioned in the text of the Constitution itself).
    *   *Includes* oath for:
        *   Candidates contesting elections (filed with nomination papers).
        *   Ministers.
        *   Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG).
        *   Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts.
*   **Schedule 4**:
    *   **Allocation of seats in the Council of States (Rajya Sabha)**.
    *   Examples:
        *   UP: 31 MPs in Rajya Sabha.
        *   Andhra Pradesh: 11 members.
        *   Telangana: 7 members.
        *   Tamil Nadu: 19 members.

#### 🏞️ 🕒 [16:38](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=998) **2.3 Schedule 5: Administration of Scheduled Areas**
*   Deals with administration of **Scheduled Areas** in states *other than* Assam, Meghalaya, Mizoram, and Tripura (AMTM).
*   There are **11 states** with Scheduled Areas.
*   **President of India** can establish a Scheduled Area in a state with a significant tribal population.
    *   Can increase, decrease, or alter boundaries of Scheduled Areas.
*   **Governor** of the state has the responsibility to administer the Scheduled Area.
*   **Tribes Advisory Council (TAC)**:
    *   Established by the Governor to administer Scheduled Areas.
    *   Composition: May have up to **20 members**.
        *   **3/4 th** shall be tribal MLAs from the state.
        *   Remaining **1/4 th** nominated by the Governor.
    *   Recommendations are **advisory**, not binding on the Governor.
*   **Purpose of Schedule 5**: Protection of the **economic interests** of tribals.
    *   Examples:
        *   Regulating sale of tribal land to non-tribals.
        *   Controlling money lending.
    *   Historical context: British policies restricted shifting agriculture (Jhum, Poda) and led to tribal uprisings.
    *   Governor has power to decide applicability of Parliamentary or State laws (related to land, land acquisition) to Scheduled Areas.

#### ⛰️ 🕒 [18:51](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=1131) **2.4 Schedule 6: Administration of Tribal Areas in AMTM States**
*   Exclusively covers tribal areas in **Assam, Meghalaya, Mizoram, and Tripura (AMTM)**.
*   **Purpose**: Protect not only economic interests but also **social customs, religious matters, family matters (divorce)** of tribals in these specific states by granting **autonomy**.
*   **Autonomous Districts**:
    *   Established for these tribal areas to govern themselves.
    *   Examples: Not entire Assam is tribal; specific districts are.
        *   Meghalaya: 3 districts (Garo, Khasi, Jaintia - also hills).
*   **District Councils**:
    *   Democratic institutions for Autonomous Districts.
    *   Composition (e.g., 30 members):
        *   **26 elected** by the tribes of that district.
        *   **4 nominated** by the Governor.
    *   **Powers**:
        *   Make laws for the district (related to social customs, land, forests, etc.).
        *   Financial powers (impose certain taxes, collect tolls).
        *   Regulate education.
        *   Regulate marriage and divorce.
*   **Autonomous Regions**:
    *   If a district has more than one tribe, Autonomous Regions can be created.
    *   These regions will have **Regional Councils** instead of District Councils.

#### 📜 🕒 [26:36](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=1596) **2.5 Schedule 7: Distribution of Legislative Subjects**
*   Divides legislative subjects into three lists:
    *   **Union List**: Parliament has exclusive power.
        *   Examples: Defence, External Affairs, Communication, Banking, Insurance, Citizenship, Extradition.
    *   **State List**: State Legislatures have exclusive power (normally).
        *   Examples: Agriculture, Gambling, Police, Law and Order, Land.
    *   **Concurrent List**: Both Parliament and State Legislatures can make laws.
        *   Examples: Education (shifted from State List by 42 nd Amendment, 1976), Marriage, Criminal Procedure, Civil Procedure.
*   In case of conflict on a Concurrent List item, Parliamentary law generally prevails (details discussed later).

#### 🗣️ 🕒 [28:28](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=1708) **2.6 Schedule 8: Languages**
*   Initially **14 languages**.
*   Currently **22 languages**.
*   **English is NOT part of the 8 th Schedule**, despite being an official language of the Union.
*   **Rajasthani is NOT part of the 8 th Schedule**.
*   **Sahitya Akademi Awards**:
    *   Recognizes the 22 languages of the 8 th Schedule.
    *   Also recognizes **English and Rajasthani** for its awards.

#### 🛡️ 🕒 [30:05](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=1805) **2.7 Schedule 9: Validation of Certain Acts and Regulations**
*   Laws placed in the 9 th Schedule are generally protected from **judicial review**.
*   Added by the **1 st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1951**.
*   **Judicial Review Status**:
    *   Laws placed *before* **24 th April 1973 (Kesavananda Bharati judgment)**: No judicial review.
    *   Laws placed *after* **24 th April 1973**: Can be judicially reviewed if they violate the basic structure.

#### 🚫 🕒 [30:47](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=1847) **2.8 Schedule 10: Anti-Defection Law**
*   Added by the **52 nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1985**.
*   Applies to **Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha, Legislative Assemblies, Legislative Councils**.
*   *Does NOT apply* to Panchayats and Municipalities (States can make their own laws for them).
*   **Grounds for Disqualification**:
    1.  **Member of a political party**:
        *   Voluntarily resigns from the party.
            *   "Voluntary resignation" can be inferred from conduct (anti-party activities, attending opposition rallies) even without a formal letter.
        *   Violates a **three-line whip** (not one-line or two-line whip).
            *   Can be condoned by the political party within **15 days** of violation; if condoned, no disqualification.
    2.  **Independent Member**:
        *   Joins any political party *after* election.
    3.  **Nominated Member**:
        *   Joins any political party *after 6 months* from the date of nomination. (Has 6 months to decide).
*   **Disqualifying Authority**:
    *   **Speaker** (Lok Sabha, Legislative Assembly).
    *   **Chairman** (Rajya Sabha, Legislative Council).
*   **Judicial Review**:
    *   Originally, the decision of the Speaker/Chairman was final.
    *   **Kihoto Hollohan vs. Zachilhu (1992)**: Supreme Court held that the decision is subject to judicial review (as judicial review is part of the basic structure, established in Minerva Mills case, 1980).
*   **Time limit for decision**: No time period specified for Speaker/Chairman to act on disqualification petitions.
*   **Disqualification of Speaker/Chairman**: If the Speaker/Chairman themselves are subject to disqualification, the House will decide who will adjudicate.

#### 🏘️ 🕒 [39:22](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=2362) **2.9 Schedules 11 & 12: Panchayats and Municipalities**
*   **Schedule 11**: Added by **73 rd Amendment (1992)** for Panchayats.
*   **Schedule 12**: Added by **74 th Amendment (1992)** for Municipalities.
*   **Historical Context**:
    *   First Panchayati Raj institution: **Nagaur district, Rajasthan (1959)**, based on **Balwant Rai G Mehta Committee** recommendations.
        *   Committee was set up to review Community Development Programs and National Extension Service.
        *   Recommended **democratic decentralization**.
    *   Local government is a **State List** subject (7 th Schedule).
    *   **National Development Council (NDC)** meeting (1958) decided to implement recommendations but no rigid pattern; uniformity lacked.
    *   Early PRIs aimed to implement **Article 40 (DPSP)** - organize village panchayats and enable them as units of rural local self-government.
    *   Self-government requires autonomy over **3 Fs**: Functions, Funds, Functionaries.
    *   **LM Singhvi Committee** recommended constitutional status for PRIs to Rajiv Gandhi.
    *   PV Narasimha Rao government gave constitutional status.
*   **Creation of PRIs and ULBs**:
    *   Created by **laws passed by State Legislatures**, not directly by Parliament for every state.
    *   State laws *must* incorporate **compulsory provisions** from 73 rd/74 th Amendments.
    *   May incorporate **voluntary provisions**.
*   **Key Compulsory Provisions (Panchayats - 73 rd Amendment)**:
    *   **Gram Sabha**: Body of all registered voters in a village. Must be established. Powers decided by State Legislature.
    *   **Three-tier structure**: Gram Panchayat (village), Panchayat Samiti (block/intermediate), Zilla Parishad (district).
        *   States with population < 20 lakhs may not create intermediate tier.
    *   **Direct elections** to all three tiers.
    *   **Reservation**:
        *   For **SCs and STs**: In proportion to their population.
        *   For **Women**: Not less than 1/3 rd (can be more, e.g., 50% in some states).
    *   **Term**: 5 years.
    *   **Minimum age to contest**: 21 years. (Lok Sabha/Assembly: 25; Council/Rajya Sabha: 30).
        *   State law can prescribe *additional* qualifications/disqualifications (e.g., education, functional toilet).
    *   **State Election Commission (SEC)**: Single-member, conducts elections to Panchayats and Municipalities.
        *   Appointed by Governor.
        *   Removed like a High Court judge (by President on resolution by Parliament by special majority).
        *   Other service conditions (tenure, qualifications) decided by State Legislature law.
            *   Example: Andhra Pradesh case - attempt to reduce SEC tenure via ordinance struck down by court as colorable legislation.
    *   **State Finance Commission (SFC)**: Constituted by Governor every 5 years.
        *   Recommends distribution of state's tax revenue with local bodies.
*   **Voluntary Provisions (Panchayats)**:
    *   Reservation for **Other Backward Classes (OBCs)**.
*   **Devolution of Powers**:
    *   **Schedule 11**: Lists **29 functions** that *may* be devolved to Panchayats by State Legislature.
        *   Examples: Agriculture, land improvement, rural electrification, drinking water, sanitation, education.
        *   Criticism: "May" instead of "shall" devolve.
    *   **Schedule 12**: Lists **18 items** that *may* be devolved to Municipalities.
*   **Entry into Force**:
    *   **73 rd Amendment (Schedule 11)**: Enacted 1992, effective **24 th April 1993**.
    *   **74 th Amendment (Schedule 12)**: Enacted 1992, effective **1 st June 1993**.
    *   *They did not enter into force together on the same day.*

### 🤝 🕒 [01:00:32](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=3632) **4.0 Part XI: Relations Between the Union and the States**

#### ⚖️ 🕒 [01:00:32](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=3632) **4.1 Legislative Relations**
*   **Territorial Jurisdiction**:
    *   **Parliament**: Can make laws for the whole or any part of India.
        *   Parliamentary laws are applicable throughout India.
        *   Parliament has power for **extra-territorial legislation** (applicable to Indian citizens and their property outside India).
    *   **State Legislature**: Laws applicable within the state.
        *   Example: Uttarakhand's UCC law applies only within Uttarakhand.
*   **Residuary Powers (Article 248)**:
    *   Subjects not mentioned in Union, State, or Concurrent lists.
    *   Power to legislate on residuary subjects rests with **Parliament**.
    *   Deviation from US model (where residuary powers are with states).
*   **Parliamentary Legislation on State List Subjects**:
    *   **Article 249**: If **Rajya Sabha** passes a resolution (by not less than 2/3 rd of members present and voting) stating it's in **national interest** for Parliament to make law on a State List item.
        *   Law valid for **1 year**.
        *   Can be extended for 1 year at a time by similar Rajya Sabha resolution.
    *   **Article 250**: During a **National Emergency (Article 352)**.
        *   Parliament assumes **concurrent power of legislation** with respect to State List items (effectively, State List becomes Concurrent List).
        *   Law valid till National Emergency continues + **6 months** after revocation.
    *   **Inconsistency with State Law (under Art 249/250)**: If Parliament makes a law under Art 249 or 250, and it conflicts with a State law on the same subject, the **Parliamentary law will prevail** under all circumstances (even if state law got President's assent).
    *   **Article 252**: If **two or more States** pass resolutions requesting Parliament to legislate on a State List matter.
        *   Parliament becomes empowered to make law for *those requesting states*.
        *   Other states can adopt this law by passing a similar resolution in their legislatures.
        *   It's like surrendering the subject to Parliament.
    *   **Article 253**: To implement **international treaties, agreements, or conventions**.
        *   Parliament can legislate on any matter (even State List) to give effect to international obligations.
        *   States cannot object on grounds of encroachment.
*   **Inconsistency between Union and State Laws on Concurrent List (Article 254)**:
    *   Generally, **Parliamentary law prevails** over State law if both legislate on a Concurrent List item and there's a conflict.
    *   **Exception**: If a State law on a Concurrent List item has been **reserved for the President's consideration and received his assent**, then that State law will prevail *in that State*.
        *   However, Parliament can still override this State law by subsequently making a law on the same matter.
*   **Requirement of Recommendation and Previous Sanction (Article 255)**:
    *   Certain bills require prior recommendation/sanction of President/Governor (e.g., Money Bill, Article 3 Bill).
    *   If such a bill is passed *without* prior sanction but *later receives the President's/Governor's assent*, the Act cannot be invalidated solely on the ground of lack of prior sanction. It's treated as a procedural lapse cured by subsequent assent.

#### ⚙️ 🕒 [01:19:31](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=4771) **4.2 Administrative Relations**
*   **Obligation of States and the Union (Article 256)**:
    *   States must ensure compliance with laws made by Parliament.
    *   Example: Citizenship Amendment Act, 2019 – States cannot refuse implementation.
    *   Centre can issue directions to States for compliance.
*   **Control of the Union over States in certain cases (Article 257)**:
    *   Centre can issue directives to States regarding:
        *   Construction and maintenance of means of communication declared to be of national or military importance (e.g., highways, waterways).
        *   Measures for the protection of railways within the State.
    *   If States incur extra costs due to these directives, Centre should pay.
        *   Disputes over payment resolved by an **arbitrator appointed by the Chief Justice of India (CJI)**. (Not Supreme Court under Art 131).
    *   Failure to comply with such directives can lead to **President's Rule (Article 356)**, as it can be deemed a breakdown of constitutional machinery (under Article 365).
*   **Delegation of Powers**:
    *   **Distribution of Power**: Subjects to Centre/States.
    *   **Separation of Power**: Legislature, Executive, Judiciary.
    *   **Devolution of Power**: Superior to inferior (e.g., States to Panchayats).
    *   **Delegation of Power**: Temporary, revocable, usually by consent.
        *   **Article 258**: President (Union Executive) can, with the consent of the State Government, entrust functions to that State.
        *   **Article 258 A**: Governor (State Executive) can, with the consent of the Government of India, entrust functions to the Union.
            *   Added by **7 th Amendment, 1956**.
            *   Enables **mutual delegation**, fostering **cooperative federalism** (concept from Australia).
        *   Delegation by Parliament (forceful): Parliament can also confer powers and impose duties upon a State or its officers, even without State consent, for Union purposes.
*   **Public Acts, Records, and Judicial Proceedings (Article 261 - Full Faith and Credit Clause)**:
    *   Public acts, records, and judicial proceedings of the Union and of every State shall be given full faith and credit throughout India.
    *   Final judgments or orders of civil courts in any part of India are executable anywhere within India.
    *   Example: Marriage certificate valid across states; warrant issued in one state executable in another.
*   **Disputes relating to Waters (Article 262 - Interstate River Water Disputes)**:
    *   Parliament may by law provide for the adjudication of disputes relating to the use, distribution, or control of waters of any inter-state river or river valley.
    *   Parliament may also provide that **neither the Supreme Court nor any other court** shall exercise jurisdiction in respect of any such dispute.
        *   Seen as a political matter, best resolved through political consensus.
        *   **Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956** enacted.
        *   Despite this, Supreme Court does hear cases (e.g., on livelihood grounds, as appellate review).
*   **Inter-State Council (Article 263)**:
    *   If it appears to the **President** that public interest would be served by establishing a council to:
        *   Inquire into and advise upon disputes between States.
        *   Investigate and discuss subjects of common interest to Union and States, or States themselves.
        *   Make recommendations for better coordination of policy and action.
    *   It is a **non-permanent body** (President creates it when needed).
    *   Established in **1990**, headed by PM, includes CMs.
    *   **Consultative/Advisory role**, not adjudicatory like tribunals under Art 262.
        *   Examples: Discuss governor's role, NEP, Finance Commission TORs.
        *   Past councils on local govt, taxes existed.

#### 💰 🕒 [01:39:04](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=5944) **4.3 Financial Relations**
*   **No tax shall be levied or collected except by authority of law (Article 265)**.
    *   Taxes cannot be imposed by executive notification; requires a law.
*   **Consolidated Fund and Public Accounts of India and of the States (Article 266)**:
    *   **Consolidated Fund of India (CFI)** / State:
        *   Constitutional fund.
        *   All revenues received by Govt. (taxes, loans raised) go into it.
        *   Operated by **Parliamentary law (Appropriation Bill)**. Executive cannot withdraw without Parliament's approval.
    *   **Public Account of India** / State:
        *   Constitutional fund.
        *   Money other than those credited to Consolidated Fund (e.g., provident fund deposits, savings bank deposits, judicial deposits, pension deposits).
        *   Operated by **executive action** (Parliamentary approval not needed for withdrawal).
*   **Contingency Fund (Article 267)**:
    *   A **statutory fund** (Parliament may by law establish it).
        *   **Contingency Fund of India Act, 1950**.
    *   Held by the President (operated by Finance Secretary on behalf of President) to meet unforeseen expenditure pending authorization by Parliament.
    *   An imprest (fixed sum, e.g., 500 crores).
    *   Withdrawals made for emergency needs (e.g., supplementary grants if Parliament not in session).
    *   Must be **replenished** by taking money from CFI (through Parliamentary approval).
*   **Distribution of Revenues between Union and States**:
    *   Various categories of taxes (imposed by Centre/collected by States, imposed by Centre/collected by Centre/shared with States like Income Tax, Corporate Tax).
*   **Finance Commission (Article 280)**:
    *   President shall constitute a Finance Commission within 2 years of Constitution's commencement, and thereafter **every 5 years**.
    *   Composition: **Chairman and 4 other members** appointed by President.
    *   **Role**:
        *   Recommend distribution of net proceeds of taxes between Union and States (vertical devolution).
        *   Recommend allocation between States of their respective shares (horizontal devolution).
        *   Principles governing grants-in-aid to States out of CFI.
        *   Measures to augment Consolidated Fund of a State to supplement resources of Panchayats/Municipalities.
        *   Any other matter referred by President in the interests of sound finance.
    *   Terms of reference decided by Central Government.
    *   Recommendations are **advisory, not binding** on the government.
    *   President must lay the **report of the Finance Commission** before Parliament, along with an **explanatory memorandum (Action Taken Report - ATR)** on the action taken on its recommendations.
*   **Exemption of Property of the Union from State Taxation (Article 285)**:
    *   Property of the Union is exempt from all taxes imposed by a State or any authority within a State, unless Parliament by law provides otherwise.
*   **Exemption from Taxes on Electricity (Article 287)**:
    *   No State law shall impose a tax on the consumption or sale of electricity if it is:
        *   Consumed by the Government of India.
        *   Sold to the Government of India.
        *   Consumed in the construction, maintenance, or operation of any railway by GoI.
    *   Parliament may by law provide otherwise.
*   **Exemption of Property and Income of a State from Union Taxation (Article 288)**:
    *   The property and income of a State shall be exempt from Union taxation.
*   **Suits and Proceedings (Article 300)**:
    *   The Government of India may sue or be sued by the name of the **Union of India**.
    *   The Government of a State may sue or be sued by the name of the **State** (e.g., Kesavananda Bharati vs. State of Kerala).
*   **Right to Property (Article 300 A)**:
    *   Previously a Fundamental Right.
    *   Made a constitutional/legal right by **44 th Amendment Act, 1978**.
    *   "No **person** shall be deprived of his property save by **authority of law**."
        *   Means property cannot be taken by executive action; requires a legislative enactment.
        *   Available to "any person" (not just citizens). (UPSC PYQ 2021 context)

### 👨‍💼 🕒 [02:15:10](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=8110) **5.0 Part XIV: Services under the Union and the States**
*   **Article 309: Recruitment and conditions of service of persons serving the Union or a State**
    *   Acts of the **appropriate Legislature** (Parliament for Union posts, State Legislature for State posts) may regulate the recruitment and conditions of service.
*   **Article 310: Tenure of office of persons serving the Union or a State**
    *   Persons serving under the **Union** hold office during the **pleasure of the President**.
    *   Persons serving under a **State** hold office during the **pleasure of the Governor**.
    *   **All India Services (AIS)**: Members (IAS, IPS, IFS) hold office during the **pleasure of the President**.
        *   Currently three AIS: Indian Administrative Service (IAS), Indian Police Service (IPS), Indian Forest Service (IFS).
*   **Article 311: Dismissal, removal or reduction in rank of persons employed in civil capacities under the Union or a State**
    *   **Safeguards**:
        1.  Cannot be dismissed or removed by an authority **subordinate** to that by which they were appointed.
        2.  Cannot be dismissed, removed, or reduced in rank except after an **inquiry** in which they have been informed of the charges and given a reasonable opportunity of being heard.
    *   **Exceptions to Inquiry**: Inquiry is NOT required if:
        1.  Dismissal/removal/reduction is based on conduct which has led to **conviction on a criminal charge**.
        2.  The authority empowered to dismiss/remove/reduce is satisfied that for some reason, to be recorded in writing, it is **not reasonably practicable to hold such inquiry**.
        3.  The **President or the Governor** is satisfied that in the interest of the **security of the State**, it is not expedient to hold such inquiry.
*   **Article 312: All-India Services**
    *   Indian Administrative Service (IAS) and Indian Police Service (IPS) were deemed services created by Parliament under this Article at the commencement of the Constitution (successors to ICS and IP).
    *   Sardar Patel is known as the **father of All India Services**.
    *   Indian Forest Service (IFS) was also created as an AIS.
    *   **Creation of New All-India Services**:
        *   Can be created by **Parliament** by law.
        *   Parliament can do so *only if* the **Rajya Sabha** declares by resolution supported by not less than **two-thirds of the members present and voting** that it is necessary or expedient in the **national interest** to do so.
        *   AIS officers are appointed/recruited by the Union, trained by the Union, accountable to the Union, but work in States (centralizing tendency, seen as against pure federalism by some).
    *   **All-India Judicial Service (AIJS)**:
        *   Provision for AIJS (not including posts inferior to District Judge) was made by the **42 nd Amendment Act, 1976**.
        *   This amendment has not been notified yet (AIJS not yet operational).
*   **Article 315: Public Service Commissions for the Union and for the States**
    *   There shall be a **Union Public Service Commission (UPSC)** for the Union.
    *   There shall be a **State Public Service Commission (SPSC)** for each State.
    *   **Joint State Public Service Commission (JPSC)**:
        *   Two or more States may agree to have one.
        *   If a resolution to that effect is passed by the House or Houses of the Legislature of each of those States, **Parliament may by law** provide for the appointment of a JPSC.
        *   JPSC is a **statutory body** (UPSC and SPSC are constitutional bodies).
        *   Resolution for JPSC must be passed by **both Houses** if a State has a bicameral legislature.
    *   UPSC may, with the approval of the **President** (if requested by the Governor of a State), agree to serve all or any of the needs of the State.
        *   Example: Haryana utilized UPSC services for some years.
*   **Article 316: Appointment and term of office of members (UPSC/SPSC/JPSC)**
    *   **Appointment**:
        *   Chairman and members of **UPSC/JPSC**: Appointed by the **President**.
        *   Chairman and members of **SPSC**: Appointed by the **Governor** of the State.
    *   **Term of Office**:
        *   **UPSC**: 6 years or until age 65, whichever is earlier.
        *   **SPSC/JPSC**: 6 years or until age 62, whichever is earlier.
*   **Article 317: Removal and suspension of a member of a Public Service Commission**
    *   Chairman or any member of UPSC, SPSC, or JPSC can be removed from office only by order of the **President**.
    *   **Grounds for Removal**:
        1.  **Misbehaviour**:
            *   President must refer the matter to the **Supreme Court** for inquiry.
            *   If SC, after inquiry, reports that the member ought to be removed, the President **shall** remove them.
        2.  **Other Grounds** (No SC inquiry needed for these):
            *   Adjudged an **insolvent**.
            *   Engages during term of office in any **paid employment outside the duties of his office**.
            *   Is, in the opinion of the President, **unfit to continue** in office by reason of **infirmity of mind or body**.
*   **Article 319: Prohibition as to the holding of offices by members of Commission on ceasing to be such members**
    *   **Chairman of UPSC**: Ineligible for further employment under Government of India or a State.
    *   **Member of UPSC**: Eligible for appointment as Chairman of UPSC or Chairman of an SPSC, but not for other government employment.
    *   **Chairman of SPSC**: Eligible for appointment as Chairman or member of UPSC, or as Chairman of any other SPSC, but not for other government employment.
    *   **Member of SPSC**: Eligible for appointment as Chairman or member of UPSC, or as Chairman of that SPSC or any other SPSC, but not for other government employment.
    *   *Purpose*: To ensure independence and impartiality.
*   **Article 322: Expenses of Public Service Commissions**
    *   Expenses of **UPSC** (salaries, allowances, pensions) are **charged on the Consolidated Fund of India**. (Non-votable by Parliament, but can be discussed).
    *   Expenses of **SPSC** are **charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State**.
*   **Article 323: Reports of Public Service Commissions**
    *   **UPSC**: Submits an annual report to the **President**.
    *   **SPSC/JPSC**: Submits an annual report to the **Governor (s)** concerned.
    *   The President/Governor shall cause the report to be laid before the **Parliament/State Legislature** respectively.
    *   Report must be accompanied by a **memorandum (Action Taken Report - ATR)** explaining cases, if any, where the advice of the Commission was not accepted and the reasons for such non-acceptance.

### 🏛️ 🕒 [02:39:27](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=9567) **6.0 Part XIVA: Tribunals**
*   Not originally part of the Constitution.
*   Added by the **42 nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976**.
*   **Purpose of Tribunals**:
    *   Quasi-judicial bodies (have judicial members and non-judicial experts).
    *   Provide swift resolution of disputes.
    *   Reduce the burden on ordinary courts.
    *   Concerns about "tribunalization" reducing sanctity of High Courts (mains topic).
*   **Article 323 A: Administrative Tribunals**
    *   Parliament may by law provide for the adjudication of disputes relating to recruitment and conditions of service of persons appointed to public services (Union, States, local bodies, corporations owned/controlled by Govt).
    *   Examples: Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT), State Administrative Tribunals (SATs).
    *   Can *only* be created by **Parliament**.
*   **Article 323 B: Tribunals for other matters**
    *   The **appropriate Legislature** (Parliament or State Legislature, depending on the subject matter) may by law provide for adjudication of disputes concerning:
        *   Taxation
        *   Foreign exchange, import/export
        *   Industrial and labor disputes
        *   Land reforms
        *   Ceiling on urban property
        *   Elections to Parliament and State Legislatures (though not set up, election petitions go to High Courts)
        *   Foodstuffs
        *   Rent, its regulation and control, and tenancy issues.
    *   Can be created by **Parliament or State Legislatures**.

### 🗳️ 🕒 [02:44:38](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=9878) **7.0 Part XV: Elections**
*   Elections are crucial for representative democracy. Must be **free, fair, and periodic**.
*   **Article 324: Superintendence, direction and control of elections to be vested in an Election Commission**
    *   **Election Commission of India (ECI)** is a constitutional body.
    *   Responsible for elections to: Parliament (Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha), State Legislatures (Assembly, Council), President, Vice-President.
    *   Presence of ECI conducting state legislature elections signifies a quasi-federal constitution with unitary bias (States don't have their own ECs for these elections, only for local bodies).
    *   **Composition**: Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) and such number of other Election Commissioners (ECs), if any, as the President may from time to time fix.
        *   Appointment by President. Constitutionally, Parliament was to make a law for appointment, which wasn't done for decades.
        *   **Anup Baranwal vs. Union of India (2023)**: SC ruled current system violated Art 14 (arbitrariness) and compromised free/fair elections. SC directed a committee (PM, LoP in Lok Sabha, CJI) for appointments until Parliament enacts a law.
        *   Parliament then enacted a law: Committee now comprises PM, LoP in Lok Sabha, and a Cabinet Minister nominated by PM (matter pending before SC).
    *   **Removal**:
        *   **CEC**: Removed in the same manner and on like grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court (proved misbehaviour or incapacity, resolution by both Houses by special majority).
        *   **ECs/Regional Commissioners**: Removed by President only on the recommendation of the CEC.
*   **Article 325: No person to be ineligible for inclusion in, or to claim to be included in a special, electoral roll on grounds of religion, race, caste or sex**
    *   Mandates a **common electoral roll**.
    *   Prohibits separate electorates (unlike Morley-Minto reforms which had communal electorates).
    *   Reservation for SCs/STs is different from separate electorates (voters are common).
*   **Article 326: Elections to the House of the People and to the Legislative Assemblies of States to be on the basis of adult suffrage**
    *   Provides for **Universal Adult Suffrage**.
    *   **Right to vote**: Considered a constitutional right by UPSC (though practically statutory via RPA 1951).
    *   Voting age: Originally 21 years. Lowered to **18 years** by the **61 st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988**.
*   **Article 327: Power of Parliament to make provision with respect to elections to Legislatures**
    *   Parliament can make laws relating to all matters concerning elections to Parliament and State Legislatures (e.g., delimitation, electoral rolls, conduct of elections).
    *   Examples: Representation of the People Act 1950, RPA 1951, Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Act.
*   **Article 328: Power of Legislature of a State to make provision with respect to elections to such Legislature**
    *   State Legislature can make laws for elections to its own Houses, *provided Parliament has not already made provisions* on that matter.
    *   Since Parliament has made comprehensive laws, Art 328 is largely uninvoked.
*   **Article 329: Bar to interference by courts in electoral matters**
    *   Validity of any law relating to **delimitation of constituencies** or allotment of seats shall not be called in question in any court.
    *   No election to Parliament or State Legislature shall be called in question except by an **election petition** presented to such authority and in such manner as provided by law (i.e., High Court).
    *   Despite this, SC has held that delimitation orders can be challenged if malafide/arbitrary, as judicial review is a basic structure.

### 🌟 🕒 [03:03:19](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=10999) **8.0 Part XVI: Special Provisions Relating to Certain Classes**
*   Focuses on classes of people, not states (state-specific special provisions are in Part XXI).
*   **Article 330**: Reservation of seats for **Scheduled Castes (SCs) and Scheduled Tribes (STs)** in the **House of the People (Lok Sabha)**.
*   **Article 330 A**: Reservation of seats for **Women** in the **House of the People**.
    *   Inserted by **106 th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2023**.
    *   *Not yet in force*; will be effective after census and delimitation.
*   **Article 331**: Representation of the **Anglo-Indian community** in the House of the People.
    *   President could nominate not more than 2 members if community not adequately represented.
    *   This provision was **discontinued** by the **104 th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2019** (did not extend it beyond 70 years).
*   **Article 332**: Reservation of seats for **SCs and STs** in the **Legislative Assemblies of the States**.
*   **Article 332 A**: Reservation of seats for **Women** in the **Legislative Assemblies of the States** (also by 106 th Amendment).
*   **Article 333**: Representation of the **Anglo-Indian community** in the Legislative Assemblies of the States (Governor could nominate one member).
    *   Also **discontinued** by the 104 th Amendment.
*   **Article 334: Reservation of seats and special representation to cease after certain period**
    *   Originally, these reservations (SC/ST, Anglo-Indian) were for **10 years** (1950-1960).
    *   Extended every 10 years.
    *   **104 th Amendment Act, 2019**:
        *   Extended SC/ST reservation for another 10 years (i.e., till **2030**, making it 80 years total).
        *   Did *not* extend Anglo-Indian representation (it ceased after 70 years in 2020).
*   **Article 335: Claims of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes to services and posts**
    *   Claims of SCs/STs shall be taken into consideration, consistently with the **maintenance of efficiency of administration**, in making appointments to services and posts.
    *   "Efficiency of administration" is **not defined** in the Constitution (UPSC PYQ 2023 context).
    *   Cited in **Indra Sawhney vs. Union of India (Mandal case)** regarding OBC reservation.
        *   Court upheld OBC reservation with conditions: prove social/educational backwardness, inadequate representation, 50% cap (unless extraordinary), creamy layer exclusion, and subject to Art 335.
*   **National Commissions**:
    *   **Article 338: National Commission for Scheduled Castes (NCSC)**.
        *   Initially, a combined National Commission for SCs and STs.
    *   **Article 338 A: National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST)**.
        *   Bifurcated from the combined commission by **89 th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003**.
    *   **Article 338 B: National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC)**.
        *   Initially a statutory body.
        *   Given **constitutional status** by **102 nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 2018**.
*   **Identification of SCs, STs, and SEBCs**:
    *   **Article 341: Scheduled Castes**
        *   **President** may, with respect to any State/UT (after consultation with Governor if it's a State), by public notification, specify the castes, races or tribes deemed SCs.
        *   **Parliament** may by law include in or exclude from this list.
    *   **Article 342: Scheduled Tribes**
        *   Similar process as for SCs: President notifies (consults Governor for States), Parliament can amend list.
        *   A community declared ST in one state need not be so in another (UPSC PYQ 2024).
    *   **Article 342 A: Socially and Educationally Backward Classes (SEBCs)**
        *   President (consults Governor for States) specifies SEBCs in the **central list**. Parliament can amend.
        *   Initially, after NCBC got constitutional status, SC interpreted that only Centre could identify SEBCs for states too.
        *   **105 th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2021** clarified/restored power to **States and UTs** to prepare and maintain their own list of SEBCs.

### 🗣️ 🕒 [03:21:04](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=12064) **9.0 Part XVII: Official Language**
*   **Article 343: Official language of the Union**
    *   Shall be **Hindi in Devanagari script**. Numerals to be international form of Indian numerals.
    *   **English** was to continue for official purposes for **15 years** from Constitution's commencement.
    *   Parliament may by law provide for use of English beyond 15 years.
        *   **Official Languages Act, 1963** provides for continued use of English indefinitely alongside Hindi.
    *   No "national language" defined in the Constitution.
*   **Article 344: Commission and Committee of Parliament on official language**
    *   President to constitute a Commission at expiry of 5 years, and then 10 years from commencement.
        *   To recommend progressive use of Hindi and restrictions on English.
    *   Commission's report to be examined by a Parliamentary Committee (30 members: 20 Lok Sabha, 10 Rajya Sabha).
    *   **First Commission (1955)**: Headed by **B.G. Kher**.
    *   Parliamentary Committee (examined Kher report): Headed by **Gobind Ballabh Pant**.
    *   Commission due in 1960 was not set up due to protests (especially from Southern states).
*   **Linguistic Federalism Aspects**:
    *   States reorganized on linguistic basis (Fazl Ali Commission).
    *   **Article 345: Official language or languages of a State**
        *   State Legislature may adopt any one or more of the languages in use in the State or Hindi as official language (s).
        *   States have freedom; language chosen need not be in 8 th Schedule.
*   **Article 346: Official language for communication between one State and another or between a State and the Union**
    *   Language authorized for use in the Union (Hindi/English).
    *   If two or more States agree, Hindi can be the official language for communication between them. (Some states like UP, Rajasthan, MP have adopted this).
*   **Article 347: Special provision relating to language spoken by a section of the population of a State**
    *   If a substantial proportion of a State's population desires use of any language spoken by them to be recognized by that State, the **President** may, if satisfied, direct that such language shall also be officially recognized.
*   **Article 348: Language to be used in the Supreme Court and in the High Courts and for Acts, Bills, etc.**
    *   All proceedings in **Supreme Court and every High Court** shall be in **English**, until Parliament by law otherwise provides.
    *   Authoritative texts of all Bills, Acts, Ordinances, orders, rules, regulations, bye-laws (Union & State level) shall be in **English**.
*   **Article 349: Special procedure for enactment of certain laws relating to language**
    *   During the first 15 years from Constitution's commencement, no Bill altering provisions of Art 348 could be introduced without President's previous sanction, and President would not sanction without considering recommendations of the Official Language Commission & Parliamentary Committee.
*   **Article 350: Language to be used in representations for redress of grievances**
    *   Every person entitled to submit a representation to any officer/authority of Union/State in **any of the languages used in the Union or in the State**, as the case may be.
*   **Article 350 A: Facilities for instruction in mother-tongue at primary stage**
    *   Duty of every State and local authority to provide adequate facilities for instruction in the **mother-tongue at the primary stage** of education to children belonging to **linguistic minority groups**.
    *   President may issue directions to any State for securing such facilities. (Failure to comply can invoke Art 365).
    *   Added by **7 th Amendment, 1956** (States Reorganisation).
*   **Article 350 B: Special Officer for linguistic minorities**
    *   There shall be a Special Officer for linguistic minorities appointed by the **President**.
    *   Duty: To investigate all matters relating to safeguards provided for linguistic minorities.
    *   To report to the President; President shall cause reports to be laid before Parliament and sent to governments of States concerned.
    *   Added by **7 th Amendment, 1956**.
*   **Article 351: Directive for development of the Hindi language**
    *   Duty of the **Union** to promote the spread of the Hindi language, to develop it so that it may serve as a medium of expression for all the elements of the composite culture of India.

### 🚨 🕒 [03:57:12](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=14232) **10.0 Part XVIII: Emergency Provisions**
*   **Article 352: Proclamation of National Emergency**
    *   **Grounds**: If President is satisfied (on written advice of Cabinet) that a grave emergency exists threatening security of India or any part due to:
        1.  **War**
        2.  **External Aggression**
        3.  **Armed Rebellion** (replaced "internal disturbance" by **44 th Amendment, 1978**).
    *   **Approval**:
        *   Must be approved by **both Houses of Parliament** by **special majority** within **30 days** (1 month).
        *   Continues for **6 months** if approved.
        *   Can be extended indefinitely, 6 months at a time, with periodic Parliamentary approval (periodic parliamentary control). No upper limit.
    *   **Revocation**:
        *   By President at any time (on Cabinet advice).
        *   Lok Sabha can pass a resolution by simple majority disapproving its continuation.
    *   "Cabinet" (PM + ministers of cabinet rank) term inserted by 44 th Amendment.
    *   **Imposition History**: 3 times (1962, 1971, 1975).
*   **Effects of National Emergency**:
    *   Executive power of Union extends to giving directions to States on any matter.
    *   Parliament can legislate on State List subjects.
*   Life of Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies can be extended by one year at a time (indefinitely during emergency, but not beyond 6 months after emergency ceases).
    *   **Impact on Fundamental Rights**:
        *   **Article 358**: If emergency due to **War or External Aggression**, Fundamental Rights under **Article 19 are automatically suspended**. Enforcement is suspended.
            *   If due to **Armed Rebellion**, Article 19 is *not* automatically suspended.
        *   **Article 359**: President may by order declare that the right to move any court for the enforcement of specified Fundamental Rights (except **Articles 20 and 21**) shall remain suspended.
            *   Article 32 (right to constitutional remedies) is suspended in relation to the specified FRs (except for Art 20, 21).
*   **Article 355: Duty of the Union to protect States against external aggression and internal disturbance**
    *   Imposes a duty on the Union to protect every State against external aggression and internal disturbance and to ensure that the government of every State is carried on in accordance with the provisions of this Constitution.
    *   Example: Article 355 was imposed in Manipur.
*   **Article 356: Provisions in case of failure of constitutional machinery in States (President's Rule)**
    *   **Grounds**: If President is satisfied (on report of Governor or otherwise) that a situation has arisen in which the government of the State cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution (breakdown of constitutional machinery).
        *   "Breakdown of constitutional machinery" not defined, but guidelines in **S.R. Bommai vs. Union of India (1994)**.
            *   Federalism is basic structure. Misuse of Art 356 violates it.
            *   Fit cases: Violation of secularism.
            *   Unfit cases: Mere corruption allegations, failure to implement election manifesto, loss in Lok Sabha elections by ruling state party.
            *   Difference between breakdown of law and order vs. Constitutional machinery.
    *   **Immediate Consequences**:
        *   Council of Ministers headed by CM is dismissed.
        *   President assumes executive functions of the State.
        *   Legislative Assembly is usually kept in **suspended animation** (not immediately dissolved). Can be revived.
        *   Dissolution of local bodies is *not* a necessary consequence.
    *   **Parliamentary Approval**:
        *   Must be approved by **both Houses of Parliament** by **simple majority** within **2 months (60 days)**.
        *   Continues for **6 months** if approved.
        *   Can be extended for a maximum period of **3 years** (6 months at a time).
        *   Beyond 1 year, extension possible only if:
            1.  A National Emergency (Art 352) is in operation in whole of India or in the whole or any part of the State.
            2.  Election Commission certifies that elections to the Legislative Assembly cannot be held.
        *   Exception: For Punjab, it was extended up to 5 years via constitutional amendments specific to Punjab.
    *   Fundamental Rights are **unaffected** during President's Rule.
    *   State budget passed by Parliament.
    *   **Imposition History**: More than 100 times. Highest in Manipur/UP; longest in J&K.
*   **Article 357: Exercise of legislative powers under Proclamation issued under Article 356**
    *   Parliament can confer on the President the power of the State Legislature to make laws, or authorize the President to delegate such power.
    *   President cannot take over powers of the High Court of the concerned state.
*   **Article 360: Provisions as to financial emergency**
    *   **Grounds**: If President is satisfied (on Cabinet advice) that a situation has arisen whereby the financial stability or credit of India or of any part of its territory is threatened.
    *   **Approval**:
        *   Must be approved by **both Houses of Parliament** by **simple majority** within **2 months (60 days)**.
        *   If approved, continues **indefinitely** until revoked by the President (on Cabinet advice). No repeated parliamentary approval needed for continuation.
    *   **Effects**:
        *   Union can give financial directions to States (observe canons of financial propriety, austerity).
        *   Salaries and allowances of all or any class of persons serving Union/State (including Judges of Supreme Court and High Courts) can be reduced.
        *   Money Bills or other financial bills of States can be required to be reserved for President's consideration.
    *   **Imposition History**: **Never** imposed in India.

### ⚙️ 🕒 [04:18:42](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=15522) **11.0 Part XIX: Miscellaneous**
*   **Article 361: Protection of President and Governors and Rajpramukhs**
    *   President or Governor is not answerable to any court for the exercise and performance of official powers and duties.
    *   No **criminal proceedings** can be instituted or continued against President/Governor during their term of office.
    *   No process for arrest or imprisonment shall issue from any court during their term of office.
    *   Civil proceedings may be instituted after giving two months' notice.
*   **Article 361 A: Protection of publication of proceedings of Parliament and State Legislatures**
    *   No person liable to civil/criminal proceedings for publishing a **substantially true report** of proceedings of either House of Parliament/State Legislature, unless publication is proved to have been made with **malice**.
    *   Does not apply to publication of proceedings of a **secret sitting**.
*   **Article 361 B: Disqualification for appointment on remunerative political post**
    *   A member disqualified under the **10 th Schedule (Anti-Defection Law)** shall also be disqualified to hold any **remunerative political post** for the duration of the period commencing from the date of disqualification till the date on which the term of his office as such member would expire or till he contests an election and is duly elected, whichever is earlier.
    *   "Remunerative political post" means any office for which salary/remuneration is paid out of public revenue.
    *   Inserted by **91 st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003**.
*   **Article 365: Effect of failure to comply with, or to give effect to, directions given by the Union**
    *   If a State fails to comply with or give effect to any directions given in the exercise of the executive power of the Union, it shall be lawful for the **President to hold** that a situation has arisen in which the government of the State cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution (i.e., grounds for **President's Rule under Art 356**).
*   **Article 367: Interpretation**
    *   General Clauses Act, 1897, shall apply for the interpretation of the Constitution unless the context otherwise requires.
        *   Example: "he" includes "she", "his" includes "her". Computation of time.
    *   Used to interpret terms like "Constituent Assembly of Jammu and Kashmir" to mean "Legislative Assembly of Jammu and Kashmir" in the context of Article 370 modifications.

### 🔄 🕒 [04:30:57](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=16257) **12.0 Part XX: Amendment of the Constitution**
*   **Article 368: Power of Parliament to amend the Constitution and procedure therefor**
    *   Original title: "Procedure for amendment of the Constitution". Changed by **24 th Amendment Act, 1971** to clarify Parliament's power.
    *   Parliament may, in exercise of its **constituent power**, amend by way of **addition, variation or repeal** any provision of the Constitution in accordance with the procedure laid down. (UPSC PYQ 2024)
    *   **Procedure**:
        1.  An amendment can be initiated only by the introduction of a **Bill** for the purpose in **either House of Parliament** (not in state legislatures).
        2.  The Bill can be introduced either by a **minister or by a private member**.
        3.  Does **not require prior permission** of the President.
        4.  Must be passed in each House by a **special majority**:
            *   A majority (i.e., more than 50%) of the **total membership** of the House, AND
            *   A majority of **two-thirds of the members of the House present and voting**.
        5.  Each House must pass the Bill **separately**. No provision for a **joint sitting** in case of disagreement.
        6.  **Federal Provisions Amendment**: If the Bill seeks to amend federal provisions, it must also be **ratified by the Legislatures of not less than one-half of the States** by a resolution to that effect passed by those Legislatures by a **simple majority**.
            *   Federal provisions requiring ratification include:
                *   Election of the President (Articles 54, 55).
                *   Extent of the executive power of the Union and the States (Articles 73, 162).
                *   Supreme Court and High Courts (Chapter IV of Part V, Chapter V of Part VI).
                *   Distribution of legislative powers (any of the Lists in the Seventh Schedule).
                *   Representation of States in Parliament (Rajya Sabha seats).
                *   Provisions of Article 368 itself.
        7.  After being passed by both Houses (and ratified by States, if required), the Bill is presented to the **President for assent**.
        8.  The President **shall give his assent** to the Bill (made mandatory by 24 th Amendment, 1971). President cannot withhold assent or return the Bill. Pocket veto not applicable.

### ⏳ 🕒 [04:40:34](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=16834) **13.0 Part XXI: Temporary, Transitional and Special Provisions**
*   **Article 370: Temporary provisions with respect to the State of Jammu and Kashmir**
    *   Originally granted special status to J&K.
    *   Limited applicability of Indian Constitution; Parliament's legislative power restricted.
    *   Other constitutional provisions applied via Presidential orders with concurrence of State Govt.
    *   **Post August 5-6, 2019**:
        *   Presidential Order C.O. 272 (August 5, 2019) made entire Indian Constitution applicable to J&K.
        *   Presidential Order C.O. 273 (August 6, 2019) declared that Article 370 shall cease to be operative except for clause (1) which now states that all provisions of the Indian Constitution apply to J&K.
    *   Article 370 is **still part of the Constitution text but is inoperative** in its original form; its character has been altered to make the Indian Constitution fully applicable.
*   **Article 371 Series: Special provisions for certain States**
    *   Reflects **asymmetrical federalism** (some states treated differently).
    *   **Art 371**: Maharashtra and Gujarat (separate development boards for Vidarbha, Marathwada, Saurashtra, Kutch).
    *   **Art 371 A**: Nagaland (protection of customary laws, land ownership; Governor's special responsibility for law and order).
    *   **Art 371 B**: Assam (committee of MLAs from tribal areas).
    *   **Art 371 C**: Manipur (committee of MLAs from hill areas).
    *   **Art 371 D & E**: Andhra Pradesh and Telangana (equitable opportunities, Central University in AP).
    *   **Art 371 F**: Sikkim (minimum Assembly seats 30, instead of 60).
    *   **Art 371 G**: Mizoram (protection of Mizo customary laws).
    *   **Art 371 H**: Arunachal Pradesh (Governor's special responsibility for law and order).
    *   **Art 371 I**: Goa (minimum Assembly seats usually 30, as per Goa, Daman and Diu Reorganisation Act, 1987, even though Art 170 generally prescribes 60).
    *   **Art 371 J**: Karnataka (special provisions for Hyderabad-Karnataka region, now Kalyana-Karnataka; e.g., Raichur, Bellary, Bidar, Gulbarga). Added by 98 th Amendment, 2012.
*   **Article 372: Continuance in force of existing laws and their adaptation**
    *   Existing laws (pre-Constitution) shall continue in force until altered, repealed or amended by a competent Legislature.
    *   Subject to other provisions of the Constitution (e.g., consistency with Fundamental Rights under Art 13 (1)).

### 🏁 🕒 [04:50:57](https://www.youtube.com/live/Nc-nu66qQGQ?feature=shared&t=17457) **14.0 Part XXII: Short Title, Commencement, Authoritative Text in Hindi and Repeals**
*   **Article 393: Short title**
    *   This Constitution may be called **the Constitution of India**.
*   **Article 394: Commencement**
    *   This Article and Articles 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 (Citizenship), 60 (Oath of President), 324 (Election Commission), 366, 367 (Definitions, Interpretation), 379, 380, 388, 391, 392, 393 came into force **at once (26 th November 1949)**.
    *   Remaining provisions came into force on **26 th January 1950** (referred to as the "commencement of this Constitution").
    *   26 th November celebrated as **Constitution Day** since 2015 (not mentioned in Constitution).
*   **Article 394 A: Authoritative text in the Hindi language**
    *   President shall cause to be published a translation of the Constitution in Hindi.
    *   This translation shall have the **same meaning** as the original English text and shall be deemed to be, for all purposes, its authoritative text in Hindi.
*   **Article 395: Repeals**
    *   The **Indian Independence Act, 1947** is repealed.
    *   The **Government of India Act, 1935**, together with all enactments amending or supplementing that Act, is repealed (except provisions relating to abolition of Privy Council jurisdiction).
*   **Summary of Constitutional Structure**:
    *   Total **25 Parts**, numbered up to 22.
    *   Over **450 Articles**, numbered up to 395 (due to insertions like 51 A, 243 A-O, etc.).

### 📝 **Final Summary of Key Constitutional Features Mentioned:**

*   The Constitution of India is a comprehensive document, meticulously structured into Parts, Articles, and Schedules.
*   Understanding the **division of powers** (legislative, administrative, financial) between the Union and States is crucial (Parts XI, XII).
*   The **amendment procedure** (Article 368) reflects a blend of rigidity and flexibility.
*   **Emergency provisions** (Part XVIII) allow the Union to assume greater control during crises, with safeguards to prevent misuse.
*   **Fundamental Rights** (Part III) are the cornerstone of individual liberty, though subject to reasonable restrictions and modifications during emergencies.
*   **Directive Principles of State Policy** (Part IV) guide the State in governance, aiming for a welfare state.
*   **Independent institutions** like the Election Commission (Part XV), UPSC (Part XIV), CAG, and Judiciary are vital for democratic functioning and accountability.
*   **Special provisions** for certain classes (Part XVI) and certain States (Part XXI) address historical injustices and unique regional needs, reflecting India's diversity and commitment to inclusive development.
*   The **Schedules** provide detailed elaborations on various constitutional provisions, making the main articles more concise.
*   The Bare Act itself, with its precise language, is an indispensable tool for understanding the nuances of Indian polity, especially for competitive examinations.
