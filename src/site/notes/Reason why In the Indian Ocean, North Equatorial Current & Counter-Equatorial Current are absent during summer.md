| The North Equatorial Current (NEC) and Counter-Equatorial Current (CEC) are absent in the Indian Ocean during summer because of the powerful southwest monsoon winds. These winds reverse the usual westward flow of water, preventing the NEC from forming and disrupting the conditions needed for the CEC. The smaller width of the Indian Ocean also plays a role.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| * **Monsoonal Winds:** The dominant southwest monsoon winds during summer (June-September) reverse the usual westward flow of water in the Indian Ocean.  These winds blow from the southwest to the northeast, disrupting the normal current patterns.<br><br>* **Disruption of NEC:**  The reversed wind pattern prevents the formation of the North Equatorial Current (NEC), which typically flows westward.<br><br>* **Suppression of CEC:** The Counter-Equatorial Current (CEC), which usually flows eastward, cannot form because the westward-flowing NEC is absent.  The conditions required for the return flow that creates the CEC are missing.<br><br>* **Smaller Basin Width:** The Indian Ocean's narrower width compared to the Pacific and Atlantic Oceans makes its currents more susceptible to disruptions from the monsoon winds.  The proximity of landmasses (Africa and Indonesia) limits the space for currents to develop fully.<br><br>* **Monsoon Currents Dominate:**  Instead of the NEC and CEC, the Southwest Monsoon Current becomes prominent, flowing eastward from the Arabian Sea to the Bay of Bengal, south of the Equator.  This represents a complete reversal of the typical current system in the Indian Ocean during the summer.<br> |
**Context:**  
North Equatorial Current (NEC) aur Counter-Equatorial Current (CEC) dono **Pacific aur Atlantic Oceans** me toh **permanent features** hain, lekin **Indian Ocean** me ye currents **seasonal** hote hain — khas kar **summer (South-West monsoon)** ke dauraan absent ho jaate hain.

---

**Samjho ye dynamic Indian Ocean mechanism:**

### 1. **Equatorial Current System Basics**

- Normally, **Trade Winds** (NE to SW) push water westward — forming **NEC**.
- Ek part uska wapas eastward chalta hai as **Counter-Equatorial Current (CEC)**.

### 2. **Indian Ocean Exception: No Permanent Trade Winds**

- Indian Ocean me **landmass (Asia)** ka strong influence hai.
- Yahan **Trade Winds symmetrical nahi hain** — monsoonal system dominate karta hai.

---

### 3. **Summer (SW Monsoon) Dynamics:**

#### a. **Reversal of Winds**

- June–September me **South-West Monsoon** winds blow karte hain.
- Ye winds **strong aur diagonally NE-directed** hote hain, i.e. from **SW to NE** — across equator.

#### b. **Disruption of Westward NEC**

- Ye SW winds water ko **eastward push** karte hain — is wajah se
    - **Westward NEC suppress ho jaata hai**.
    - **CEC establish nahi ho pata** — kyunki equatorial return flow ki condition hi missing hai.

#### c. **Ek aur factor: Absence of Large Basin Width**

- Pacific aur Atlantic me equator ka **broad longitudinal width** hota hai — jo NEC/CEC establish hone deta hai.
- Indian Ocean me landmass (Africa–Indonesia) close hai — **zonal scale chhoti hai**, currents easily distort ho jaate hain.

---

### 4. **Instead: Monsoon Currents Form**

- Summer me:
    - **Southwest Monsoon Current** flows from **Arabian Sea → Bay of Bengal** via **Equator ke South**.
    - Indian Ocean me **reversal of current system** dekhne ko milta hai.

---

### Conclusion:

**"Indian Ocean me NEC aur CEC summer me isliye absent hain kyunki monsoonal wind reversal westward flow ko disrupt kar deta hai, aur zonal scale chhota hone se equatorial current system develop nahi ho pata."**
